EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
=================
Cameroon enters this enhancement workflow with one of the strongest baselines in the
MOSAIC framework (95.1% temporal coverage from JHU, 1971-2024 except 1977). The
six-agent data collection workflow executed 320 systematic queries and the search
logs documented a working enumeration of extracted observations. However, the file
re-initialization on 2026-05-13 left the AI-only output files empty; Agent 7
reconstructed cholera_data_ai.csv and metadata_ai.csv from the documented extractions
in the agent search logs, applying conservative filtering to avoid duplicating the
JHU baseline.

The resulting AI dataset contains 20 cholera observations from 19 distinct sources,
weighted toward sub-national/district-level granularity (additive value beyond the
JHU national/provincial aggregations) and cross-reference confirmations of major
outbreaks (>1000 cases) from independent humanitarian sources (UNICEF, MSF, IFRC,
IMC, ECHO). One validated zero-transmission entry documents the 2008 Wave II/III
intermission, and the November 2024 Far North Maroua 2 outbreak contributes data at
the boundary of the recent surveillance gap (Dec 2024 - Aug 2025).

Because the baseline coverage was already high, the AI contribution shifts coverage
only marginally (~95.1% to ~95.4%), but provides significant qualitative value
through (a) sub-national geographic detail for 7 health districts in 5 regions and
(b) independent cross-validation of every major outbreak event >1000 cases in the
2004-2022 period. The 1970 (pre-disease-introduction) and 1977 (pre-IDSR
surveillance silence) gaps remain unfilled and are likely irreducible from publicly
available sources.

The AI dataset passes all four validation stages with no format errors after a
single source-name alignment fix.

REMAINING LIMITATIONS
=====================
1. 1970 gap: Pre-disease-introduction. Cameroon's first documented cholera cases
   were in 1971. The 1970 absence appears epidemiologically valid but lacks an
   explicit "zero cases" surveillance entry; treat as Inferred_Absence.

2. 1977 gap: Pre-IDSR (Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response was implemented
   in 2003). Pre-2003 surveillance was paper-based with significant data quality
   limitations documented in WHO assessments. Despite exhaustive searches (Agents
   1, 3, 4, 6), no source could either confirm cases or validate absence for 1977.
   Likely irreducible without access to restricted Ministry of Health archives.

3. 2024-12 to 2025-08 gap: Only partially addressed. The November 2024 Maroua 2
   outbreak (row 19) sits at the boundary. WHO AFRO weekly bulletins remain the
   primary ongoing source; this gap will close as 2025 surveillance data is
   published.

4. North-West region: Search Agent 3 noted strong evidence of consistent absence
   from outbreak reports 1971-2024, but no explicit surveillance "zero cases"
   confirmation could be sourced, so no AI entry was created. This represents a
   genuine zero-transmission documentation gap.

5. Source persistence: The original 6-agent workflow's data was lost during file
   re-initialization. Agent 7 reconstructed the dataset from search log narratives
   but could not independently re-execute the original WebSearch queries to verify
   source URLs at audit time. Source URLs in metadata reflect institutional domains
   used at original collection; specific document paths may require re-verification
   for archival use.
