PART A - THE GOVERNING FINDING, ESTABLISHED FIRST AND USED THROUGHOUT
================================================================================
Ethiopia's ABSENCE FROM WHO'S ANNUAL CHOLERA TABLE IS NOT EVIDENCE OF ZERO
CASES. This was established empirically, not assumed, and it decides most of
what follows.

  * WER "Cholera, 2015" (91(38):432-440) lists the African reporting countries
    (Burundi, Cameroon, Cote d'Ivoire, DRC, Ghana, Kenya, Malawi, Mozambique,
    Niger, Nigeria, Somalia, South Sudan, Tanzania, Togo, Uganda, Zimbabwe).
    Ethiopia is not among them. EPHI's own national line-list records 227
    cholera cases and 2 deaths in Ethiopia in 2015.
  * WER "Cholera, 2016" (92(36):521-530) lists 17 African countries. Ethiopia
    is not among them. EPHI's line-list records 29,871 cases and 186 deaths in
    Ethiopia in 2016.

Two consecutive years in which Ethiopia had tens of thousands of documented
cholera cases and appears NOWHERE in WHO's annual table. Any zero row built on
"Ethiopia is not listed, therefore zero" is therefore unsound by construction.

Supporting structural facts:
  * WHO GHO (CHOLERA_0000000001/2/3) has Ethiopia entries ONLY for 1970, 1971,
    1982, 2004, 2005, 2006, 2007, 2008, 2009 and 2010. Every other year is
    ABSENT from the series. An absent year means WHO received no return; it is
    not a zero. Only 1982 and 2005 carry an EXPLICIT zero, which is a nil
    return and is a different thing entirely.
  * The WER annual cholera tables of the 1970s and 1980s list only INFECTED
    countries ("Altogether, 34 countries were infected" - WER 1981), so
    non-listing in that era cannot distinguish a nil return from no return.
  * Ethiopia's National Cholera Elimination Plan (Clin Infect Dis
    2024;79(Suppl 1):S16, PMC11244260): "Finding data from 2015 and backward
    was challenging as data had not been adequately stored in the National
    Data Center."
  * Ethiopia used "acute watery diarrhoea" in place of "cholera" before 2019
    (EPHI/IVI, Clin Infect Dis 2024;79(Suppl 1):S8: "The term 'AWD' instead of
    cholera was used before 2019"). Every candidate absence year was therefore
    stress-tested against AWD as well as cholera. One candidate (2011) failed
    that test and was retracted by this agent; see Part B.

PART D - THE ONE I GOT WRONG, AND HOW IT WAS CAUGHT
================================================================================
Index 397 asserted a national zero for 2011 on WER "Cholera, 2011": "A sharp
increase in cases reported from the Horn of Africa was observed, with 77 636
cases ... from Somalia and 127 cases ... from Djibouti. No cases were reported
from Ethiopia, Sudan or Uganda." That is an affirmative, country-named WHO
statement, corroborated by EPHI/IVI Table 1 (2011 = 0) and by evidence that
EPHI's PHEM system was collecting woreda-level cholera/AWD data from 2010
(BMC Public Health 2024;24:697, PMC10913215). It was committed at confidence
0.8 with the Somalia-77,636 caveat on the row.

A later operation in the same run found WHO's own contemporaneous field
document contradicting it. WHO/HSE/GAR/DCE/2011.3, "Public health risk
assessment and interventions - The Horn of Africa: drought and famine crisis,
July 2011" (https://iris.who.int/handle/10665/70691), Table 3 "Current outbreak
reported in situation updates from affected countries as of 15 July 2011":

    Ethiopia   Measles; meningitis; acute watery diarrhoea in parts of
               Afar, Somali and Oromia regions
    Somalia    Cholera in Mogadishu & Lower Shebele; ...

WHO wrote "cholera" for Somalia and "acute watery diarrhoea" for Ethiopia in
one table on one date - the documented Ethiopian naming convention. So the WER
sentence records a REPORTING zero while Ethiopia had AWD outbreaks in three
regions. The row was retracted by the agent that wrote it, and the presence is
recorded instead in ./data/ETH/cholera_presence_ai.csv for Afar, Somali and
Oromia (2011-07-01..2011-07-15, source 90, no case count published).

The same stress test was then applied to 2005 and 2005 PASSED - in 2004-2010
Ethiopian AWD outbreaks demonstrably DID reach WHO under the cholera heading
(WER 81(41):385-386 records the Ethiopian MoH notifying 22,101 AWD cases and
219 deaths as of 28 Sep 2006 with V. cholerae O1 Inaba confirmed). The
reporting relationship lapsed only after 2010.
