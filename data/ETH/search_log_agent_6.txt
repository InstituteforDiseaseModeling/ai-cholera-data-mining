FINDING 9 - WHAT THE SURVEILLANCE SYSTEM LOOKED LIKE, WINDOW BY WINDOW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1972-1981. The notification channel demonstrably existed and was used from 1974
(measles, pertussis), 1977 (diphtheria) and 1980 (polio). Ethiopia had itself
notified 850 cholera cases in 1970 and 2,187 in 1971, so cholera was on the
notifiable list and had been used. The decade covers the 1974 revolution, the
Derg, and the 1977-78 Ogaden war. Nothing in the record states that Ethiopia
had no cholera; the record states that Ethiopia stopped saying.

1983-1992. Surveillance genuinely degraded: 1983-85 famine, the northern wars,
and state collapse in 1991 - Ethiopia's measles notification falls to 497 in
1991, its lowest of the century, and pertussis and diphtheria returns stop
after 1990. Presence, however, is positively documented. Coppo et al., Am J
Trop Med Hyg 1995;53(4):351-9 (PMID 7485686, registered as metadata Index 140):
"During a cholera epidemic in 1985 and 1986 ... A drug-susceptible Ogawa strain
FROM ETHIOPIA caused the introduction of the disease into northern Somalia."
Recorded as a national presence record for 1985-01-01..1986-12-31 with the
inference stated plainly inside the quote: the paper's fieldwork is Somali, it
gives no Ethiopian date, place or count, and the window used is the Somali
epidemic's own span.

1993-1997 and 1998. Presence is documented (Scrascia, already in
cholera_presence_ai.csv for 1993-10..1995-12 and for 1998) and notification is
proved absent by Finding 7. Agent 4's discovery stands and now has a mechanism:
WHO/EMC/DIS/97.11 records that at WHO's urgent interregional cholera meeting in
Nairobi on 18-19 December 1997, "Djibouti, Kenya, Somalia, Tanzania and Uganda
have reported cholera outbreaks to WHO" - Ethiopia is absent from that list
although it sent a delegation, and the meeting minuted that the countries
present said there were no political constraints on notifying WHO.

1999-2003. Three independent strands.
  (a) Notification: yellow-fever nil returns filed in every year 1999-2003 and
      polio nil returns in 2002 and 2003; cholera blank in all five.
  (b) Surveillance on the ground, from the 11 August 2000 UN assessment of
      Gode, Fik, Korahe, Jijiga and Shinile zones of the Somali Region
      (https://reliefweb.int/report/ethiopia/report-food-and-nutrition-situation-gode-fik-korahe-jijiga-and-shinile-zones-somali):
      "There is no formal nutritional surveillance system covering the Somali
      region." "There is no comprehensive mortality surveillance in place in
      the region." "Prolonged diarrhoeal illness particularly among
      malnourished children has been reported frequently; cases of bloody
      diarrhoea have also been reported, but laboratory testing has been
      negative for shigella dysenteriae thus far." "Some of this decline is
      likely to be due to high mortality among those with severe malnutrition
      particularly if associated with measles or WATERY DIARRHOEA."
      In the region that would later carry Ethiopia's largest cholera burden,
      during the 2000 famine, there was no surveillance system, watery
      diarrhoea was contributing to a mortality peak, and the only laboratory
      testing mentioned was for shigella.
  (c) Context: the Ethio-Eritrean war ran May 1998 - December 2000 and the
      Gode famine peaked in 2000.
  NOTHING in this window affirmatively states zero cholera cases. The one
  quantitative fact pointing the other way is that when Ethiopia resumed
  cholera notification it reported a very small number - 16 cases for 2004 -
  and then a formal nil for 2005. That is compatible with genuinely low
  transmission in the early 2000s, and it is the reason this window is
  classified UNDETERMINED rather than NON-REPORTING outright.
