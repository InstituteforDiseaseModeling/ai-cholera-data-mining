EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
-----------------
Bottom line. Ghana now has a large, well-sourced and largely internally consistent AI layer: 2,402 rows from 662
registered sources, against 641 rows and 244 sources at cycle start. The validator passes (ERROR 0). An 82-row source
spot-check found 1 value error (a wrong period start, fixed). Two caveats limit what the work delivers to the model,
and they matter more than the volume:

  1. The headline coverage figure flatters Ghana. Month coverage is 99.7% (680/682 months), but only 50.4% of
     national months (343/681) have a national observation of 100 days or less. That is up from 31.9% at cycle start
     and 17.5% in the JHU+WHO baselines alone. The other half of Ghana's history rests on half-year or annual totals.
     The longest stretches without sub-quarterly national data are Jul 1984 - Jun 1991 (84 months) and Dec 2005 -
     Nov 2008 (36 months).
  2. Most of the AI layer's weekly detail never reaches the MOSAIC weekly series. py/build_weekly_timeseries.py
     ranks layers before methods (WHO > JHU > AI), so a JHU annual total spread across weeks overwrites an AI weekly
     observation. In a read-only re-implementation of the builder's logic, 323 of 375 AI national weekly rows never
     reach the series (309 of them overwritten by spread JHU annual totals). Only 17 of 329 positive AI weekly
     observations reach it. All the 1970s WER weeks, all the
     2000-2003 WHO AFRO weekly tables and nearly all the 2012/2015 GHS weekly series are hidden. The same builder,
     combined with WHO baseline zero-weeks stored as blanks, roughly DOUBLES the 2025 national series (about 4,900
     simulated vs WHO's final 2,442). AI outbreak-cumulative rows are spread into the blank weeks. Separately, a JHU
     cumulative row places about 2,000 phantom cases in Apr-Oct 2024, before the outbreak began. These are builder
     and baseline defects, not AI data errors. The AI rows carry do-not-sum notes, but the builder does not read
     notes. Until the builder is fixed, more AI collection for Ghana will add little to the model.
