1.1 Bottom line
The Gambia is one of the lowest-transmission countries in the MOSAIC set. Reported cholera since 1970 consists of 1971 (3 cases,
1 death), 1985 (2 imported per WHO; the press reported 4 hospitalised in Serrekunda), 1994 (1), 1995 (15, all imported), 1996 (7),
2005 (214 cases, 13 deaths - the only true epidemic), 2007 (12/1) and 2008 (1). Suspected cases that were never laboratory-confirmed
were also recorded in 2018 Q1 (8, WAHO regional bulletin), 2020 (2, Ministry of Health) and 2021 (3, Ministry of Health). There has
been no laboratory-confirmed cholera since 2008. The rest of the AI layer is evidence of absence, and after this audit only 20 of its
35 zero rows still qualify as documented, and 13 of those 20 are sub-national (7 national).

1.2 What this cycle changed
- The previous cycle's main finding has been withdrawn. That was a single zero row for 2008-2024 at confidence 0.9, which the May 2026
  report called "confirmed zero-transmission" for 2009-2022. It was wrong for 2018, 2020 and 2021. The row was retracted and replaced
  year by year, which recovered 13 suspected cases it had zeroed out: 8 in 2018 Q1, 2 in 2020 and 3 in 2021 (the 2021 cases are also in
  JHU). The rebuild also left 2019 and 19 Sep-31 Dec 2022 as honestly unknown for suspected cases.
- Agents 1-6 added 61 rows; 2 were later retracted by agents in the same run, leaving 59 live. All 5 legacy rows were retracted with
  tombstones: a duplicate of JHU 2005; an 1869 row whose "CFR" was deaths divided by population; the blanket zero; and two
  list-exclusion zeros replaced by stronger sources. 62 sources were registered, for 70 in total. Of these, 37 are cited by live rows:
  17 Level 1, 14 Level 2 and 6 Level 3.
- Only 15 of the 59 live rows carry a positive suspected count, and only 3 of those (1971, 2018 Q1, 2020) change the national weekly
  series; the rest are sub-national or sit inside JHU years. The remaining 44 rows are: 35 zero rows (20 Documented_Absence,
  15 Inferred_Absence), 5 "no outbreak" rows (cCh=0 with sCh blank), 3 deaths-only rows and 1 confirmed-count lower bound.

1.4 Gaps still open, and whether the data exist
Using the suspected-count definition, five periods remain open: Jan-Feb 1975, Dec 1978, 1982-1984, all of 2019, and 19 Sep-31 Dec
2022 (about 4.5 years in total). 21 Sep-3 Oct 2026 is also uncovered, but that is publication lag.
- 2019 and Q4 2022: the data almost certainly exist in Gambia's weekly IDSR/DHIS2 records and in the WAHO regional DHIS2. Both need a
  formal data request, not a web search.
- 1975, Dec 1978 and 1982-84: a record would exist only in the Gambian Medical and Health Department's undigitised annual reports or
  in WHO archives. 1982 may never have been compiled, because reporting to WHO lapsed in 1982-85.
Of the 7 baseline gap periods, none is closed entirely with strong evidence:
- 2 are closed or mostly closed with mixed strong and moderate evidence: 2016-2020 (except suspected cases in 2019), and 2025 to
  20 Sep 2026.
- 2 are only partly closed: 1970-1984, where only 1970-72 is strong; and 2022.
- 3 are closed only with weak inferred zeros: 1986-1993, 1997-2004 and 2009-2010.

1.7 Modelling caveats (details in A12)
(1) py/build_weekly_timeseries.py uses national rows only and lets JHU overwrite AI week by week. AI detail inside JHU years (1985,
    1994-96, 2005-08, 2011-15, 2021, 2023-24) therefore does not change the national series.
(2) The builder skips blank-sCh rows and fills unsourced historical weeks with assumed_zero at confidence 0.5. The 233 weeks left
    deliberately empty or carried only by cCh-0 rows (Jan-Feb 1975, Dec 1978, 1982-84, 2019, Oct-Dec 2022) will therefore enter the
    model as zeros, weighted higher than the 0.35-0.45 inferred zeros of 1973-1993. Mask these weeks if they should be treated as
    unknown.
(3) JHU zero years (2006, 2023, 2024 - WHO zero reports) carry no evidence label, so the builder caps them as inferred zeros at 0.6.
    AI's WHO zero report for 2025 is a documented zero at 0.9.

A12. MODELLING CAVEATS
  (a) Source priority: py/build_weekly_timeseries.py ranks WHO > JHU > AI and replaces AI weeks wherever JHU has a row. JHU's annual
      totals are spread over each year by the Fourier template, so AI timing (e.g. September 2005, June 1985, 4 Dec 1995, 1 Jan-10 Nov
      1994, Jan-Aug 2008) is lost in the national series.
  (b) National rows only: sub-national rows never enter the national series. That covers the 12 regional zeros of 2020-21 and the
      West Coast, North Bank, Banjul and Kanifing case rows.
  (c) Blank-sCh rows are skipped. cCh-0 rows (55, 58, 59, 61, 64), deaths-only rows (23, 50, 60) and the cCh>=20 row (52) add no
      suspected count. Unsourced weeks up to 52 weeks before the latest observation become assumed_zero (sCh 0, conf 0.5): 233 weeks
      here, outranking the inferred zeros weighted 0.35-0.45.
  (d) Evidence labels: JHU rows carry none, so JHU's WHO zero-report years (2006, 2023, 2024) become inferred zeros capped at 0.6.
  (e) Same-source overlap: Index 25 and 26 are nested Sep-2005 cumulatives; the builder keeps the higher weekly value, which would
      inflate Sep 2005 if JHU 2005 were ever removed.
  (f) Low-weight zeros flagged UNCERTAIN (1973; Jan-Feb 1979; 1981; 1986-88; 1997) are candidates to drop in sensitivity runs.
