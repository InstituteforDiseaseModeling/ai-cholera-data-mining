    JHU+AI. Every Guinea-Bissau epidemic year (1986-87, 1994-98, 2002-09, 2012-14) was already in the WHO annual
    series. The AI case rows add TIMING (weekly/monthly points) and GEOGRAPHY (all 9 ADM1, 283 sector/district rows),
    not new outbreaks.
 2. The coverage gain is almost all absence evidence, much of it weak. Coverage rose from 48.5% (JHU only) to 98.1%,
    but all 337 AI-only months rest on national ZERO rows: 157 of them (47%) on rows weighted <= 0.35, 69 on 0.36-0.50,
    107 on 0.51-0.70 and only 4 on rows above 0.70. Read the 1970-1993 coverage as "no recognised epidemic", not as
    "zero cases reported" (CAUTION list below).
 3. One gap stays open: 1973 (365 days). It is a SURVEILLANCE GAP, not an absence, and the weekly builder renders all
    52 weeks of 1973 as assumed zeros.
 4. Most of the AI layer's timing never reaches the MOSAIC weekly series (read-only builder check): 0 of 64 exact HDX
    weekly rows (2012-14) are used; JHU Fourier spreads place 51,506 cases inside AI-documented zero windows; 2006
    renders 21,531 cases against WHO's 37; ~11,000 of 1994's cases are placed before the October 1994 onset.
