THE METHOD THAT DID WORK, FOR WHOEVER MINES ANOTHER COUNTRY
  Enumerate a WER volume through the IRIS DSpace discovery API by exact title
  ("Weekly Epidemiological Record, {year}, vol. {year-1925}, {n} [full issue]"),
  fetch every issue, pdftotext -layout, then extract the country block in a
  COLUMN BAND anchored at the country name's own x-position - never across the
  full line, because these pages are two- and three-column and a full-line read
  mixes diseases and countries.
  Then apply three gates before believing anything:
    (a) the week grid. Find the year's first Sunday and check every extracted
        period against it. OCR turns "28.VI-4.VII" into "2S.VM.VU"; the grid
        decodes it and rejects a misread.
    (b) the annual reconciliation. Sum the year and compare with the published
        annual. It caught a smallpox table in 1977, a plague table in 1978-81,
        a 500 that belongs to no column in 1978, and an AIDS table in 1988.
    (c) a positive control before accepting any absence. Angola or Tanzania
        should appear in most issues of an African cholera volume; if they do
        not, the converter has failed and the absence is about the converter.
        I skipped this gate once, in batch 6, and published a false negative
        for 1992 that I had to retract in batch 10.
  And match the country name CASE-INSENSITIVELY for narrative items and for
  tables after 1991, CASE-SENSITIVELY for tables before that. The WER switched
  Kenya from KENYA to Kenya in 1992. Getting this wrong cost me the entire
  1992-2001 seam on the first pass - 29 of the 195 rows in this run.
