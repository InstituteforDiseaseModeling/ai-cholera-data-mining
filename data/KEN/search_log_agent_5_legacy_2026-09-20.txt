-- ADJUDICATION A3 (CONFIRMED - Agent 4's identification is correct) ------------
  Claim under test: WER 1975 no.21 (23 May 1975), Table 1 "CHOLERA - CASES AND DEATHS
  REPORTED AS OF 30 APRIL 1975", contains an unlabelled row that is Kenya's, giving
  2,371 cases / 57 deaths for 1 January - 30 April 1975, against a WHO annual for the
  whole of 1975 of 1,093. Agent 4 identified it arithmetically after the scan lost the
  row label. Three independent checks, none of which Agent 4 used, all confirm it:
  (1) ROW-INTERNAL ARITHMETIC. The 17 printed weekly values
      117 186 112 117 239 147 351 396 126 134 119 85 33 75 45 49 40
      sum to exactly 2,371, the printed row total. The row is internally coherent.
  (2) TABLE-LEVEL CLOSURE. The Africa sub-table's printed total (OCR "S 365") is
      5,365, and the listed African rows - Angola 36, Cape Verde 20, Chad 0,
      Comoros 2,675, Dahomey 0, [orphan A] 2,371, [orphan B] 166, Mozambique 3,
      Nigeria 1, South Africa 89, Togo 3, Uganda 1 - sum to exactly 5,365. The
      partition closes with no residual, so no country is missing and the orphan rows
      sit exactly where Kenya falls, between Dahomey and Mozambique.
  (3) IDENTITY COLUMN. The same physical line carries the row's 1974 full-year cell:
      402 cases / 20 deaths. WER 1975 no.29 ("Cholera in 1974") independently prints
      "Kenya .... 402" and no other African country has 402 for 1974 (Ghana 468,
      Liberia 465, Malawi 1228, Mali 130, Dahomey 75). The match is unique and exact.
  (4) NARRATIVE. The prose of the same issue says the Kenyan outbreaks "began in
      December 1974", produced "a large number of cases", that "the weekly totals have
      been steadily declining since the last week of February", and that Comoros and
      Kenya together "account for almost the totality of the cases reported by the
      African continent". Orphan A peaks at week 8 (396, last week of February) and
      declines thereafter, and 2,675 + 2,371 = 5,046 = 94% of the African total.
  VERDICT: CONFIRMED. The row is Kenya's.

  WHAT THE DISCREPANCY ACTUALLY IS - and it is NOT confined to Kenya's 1975:
    year   WHO weekly provisional notifications   WHO annual review   current WHO GHO / JHU
    1971   356 (Agent 4, WER vol.46)              301 (WER 1972 no.30, p.282)   239
    1975   2,371 in 1 Jan-30 Apr ALONE            1,093 for the whole year      1,093
    1976   436 + 1,351 = 1,787                    1,359 (WER 1977 no.19)        1,359
  The annual notification is lower than the contemporaneous weekly notification total
  in all three years, by 31% (1976), 49% (1971) and at least 117% (1975). 1971 was
  revised DOWN TWICE: 356 notified weekly, 301 in the 1972 annual review, 239 in the
  series WHO publishes today (GHO CHOLERA_0000000001, retrieved this run). JHU's 239
  is therefore a faithful copy of the current WHO annual series, not a JHU
  transcription error - the revision happened inside WHO.
  Deaths move the OTHER way in 1975: 57 by 30 April, 74 for the year. So the annual is
  not a truncated series; the case counts were genuinely revised downward while the
  death counts continued to accumulate.
  CONTROL CASE, showing this is not a generic scanning or units artefact: Comoros
  appears in the SAME Table 1 with 2,675 for 1 Jan-30 Apr 1975 and 2,675 in the annual
  review for the whole of 1975 - identical, no revision at all. The downward revision
  is country-specific.
  IMPLICATION FOR MOSAIC, stated plainly: any baseline built on the WHO annual cholera
  series (which is what JHU UID 321 is) understates Kenya's 1971, 1975 and 1976
  epidemics relative to WHO's own contemporaneous weekly notifications, and the
  understatement for 1975 is at least a factor of 2.2 on cases. This is a property of
  the WHO annual series, not of Kenya, and the same test should be run for other
  countries before the annual series is treated as ground truth anywhere.
  ACTION TAKEN: not a new row - Agent 4's 1975 weekly rows already carry both figures
  at forced confidence 0.7. The finding is recorded here, in the workflow state, and
  as an appended note on the anchor rows (see batch 3).

ITEM 1 - same_period_conflict: RESOLVED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Embakasi South (Nairobi), calendar 2017: Index 86 gives 140 cases (Mageto/Nyataya et al. 2025),
  Index 538 gives 93 (Boru et al. 2023). Both are Level 1 peer-reviewed analyses of the SAME
  Kenya MoH line list, so source tier does not separate them - completeness does. Mageto uses
  the line list as received; Boru's "clean data" sheet discards every record whose sub-county
  could not be resolved, and its own S1 Text Table A puts the discarded Nairobi records for 2017
  at 292. Ngere et al. 2024, working the same Nairobi 2017 line list, independently reports that
  339 of 2,737 records (12.4%) had to be excluded. RESOLUTION: 140 is primary; 93 is a lower
  bound. Index 538 is now marked NON-PRIMARY in its processing_notes, per the check's own
  instruction, and its confidence cut 0.70 -> 0.40. It is not deleted, because it is one cell of
  a 295-row matrix whose internal consistency would break if a single value were removed.
  The WARN itself persists: the check fires unconditionally on any same-location, same-period
  disagreement and has no test for whether the disagreement has been adjudicated.

ITEM 2 - the 2020 mechanism: STILL NOT PROVEN, and my 2024 proof was WRONG
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  (a) THE 2024 ARGUMENT IS WITHDRAWN. My batch-2 adjudication said Kenya's 302 annual cases for
      2024 "cannot be a raw suspected-case count because 267 were culture-tested and 265 were
      positive". Re-reading WER "Cholera, 2024" Table 3 against its FULL column header shows the
      Rapid diagnostic test block comes FIRST and Culture/PCR SECOND. The Kenya row reads: RDT
      tested 267, RDT positive 265, RDT positivity 99.3%; CULTURE/PCR tested 24, positive 15,
      positivity 62.5%. Only 15 of the 302 were culture or PCR confirmed - not 265. cCh on
      Index 211 is corrected from 265 to 15. AFRO's "Eight (8) cases confirmed by culture, and
      76 RDT positive" for the 2024 second wave is entirely consistent with that profile, so the
      two streams test the same way and the "different quantities" argument collapses.
      WHAT SURVIVES: the annual IS a person-level line list - WER Table 4 decomposes Kenya into
      166 male + 136 female = 302 for 2024, 1,811 + 1,714 = 3,525 for 2022 and 4,637 + 4,172 =
      8,809 for 2023, all exact - and per-case test status is known for 88% of the 302. That
      supports de-duplication and case-definition filtering at the annual-reporting step, but it
      does not prove it. Index 187 re-weighted 0.60 -> 0.75; the two 2024 figures are now carried
      as two uncertain measurements, not one settled and one superseded.
  (b) THE 2024 ROUTE IS UNAVAILABLE FOR 2020, AS A FACT ABOUT THE DOCUMENT. WER "Cholera, 2020"
      (WER 2021 no.37) contains ONLY Table 1 (cases and deaths by country) and Table 2
      (zero-reporting countries). The laboratory-testing annex and the gender/age annex were
      first published in the 2021 report, WER 2022 no.37. There is no per-case test status and no
      sex decomposition for any country for 2020. No amount of further searching changes this.
  (c) BUT THE 2020 CONFLICT IS NOW RESOLVED ANYWAY - IN THE OPPOSITE DIRECTION TO MY BATCH-2
      CONCLUSION. Boru et al.'s S1 Dataset, the Kenya MoH sub-county line list and a source
      entirely independent of WHO AFRO, sums for calendar 2020 to 710 on its clean sheet and 711
      on its extrapolated sheet. 711 is EXACTLY AFRO's frozen 2020 figure, and the composition
      matches county for county: Marsabit 365, Turkana 279, Garissa 55, Murang'a 7, Wajir 4 -
      precisely and only the five counties AFRO Week 30 2020 names in prose. Two independent
      derivations of the national line list agree to one case. The WHO annual of 447 is therefore
      264 cases BELOW Kenya's own line list, not a validated version of it. It is not a
      truncation (AFRO covers 1 Jan - 28 Aug 2020 and the epidemic had ended) and not a county
      omission (no subset of the five county totals sums to 447). My batch-2 statement that "the
      WHO annual should drive modelled annual incidence" for 2020 is WITHDRAWN. Index 492 raised
      0.60 -> 0.85, Index 190 raised 0.60 -> 0.75, both with the reversal written into their notes.
  PLAINLY: the MECHANISM behind Kenya's 447 for 2020 is not established and, absent a document
  that does not appear to exist, cannot be. What IS established is that 447 is the outlier.
