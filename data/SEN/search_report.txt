EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
-----------------
Bottom line: the national series for Senegal 1970-2026 is now well supported by verified sources.
Several known defects remain in the weekly modelling input, however, and these must be fixed in
py/build_weekly_timeseries.py, not in the data.

What changed this cycle. The dataset grew from 29 rows and 21 sources (May 2026) to 1,479 rows
and 338 sources in ./data/SEN/cholera_data_ai.csv and ./data/SEN/metadata_ai.csv.
- Rows: 1,147 case rows and 332 zero (absence) rows. 347 are national, 830 regional and 302 at
  district, department or DSS level.
- Sources: 317 of the 338 are new this cycle (Level 1: 232, Level 2: 45, Level 3: 38, Level 4: 2).
  75 registered sources carry no data row (corroboration and leads only).
- Retractions: 57 rows are tombstoned in ./data/SEN/retracted_rows_ai.csv. 8 of them are mine.
- Coverage: rose from 93.4% (637/682 months, 5 gap periods) to 99.9% (681/682). The only
  uncovered month is October 2026, the current month. All 5 gap periods are closed.

Read the coverage figure with care. Senegal has no JHU or WHO baseline file, so the baseline-only
coverage is 0% and every covered month is AI-only (681). Of those months:
- 75 national months rest only on inferred zeros: 1978 (11), 1979 (5), 1990-93 (48),
  1997 (10) and 2026 (1).
- 1989 is covered only by sub-national rows (Niakhar DSS and a Dakar laboratory series). There
  is deliberately no national row for 1989, because cholera was present but uncounted.

Positive months fell from 272 to 261. The old figure was inflated by long aggregate rows. 56 of
the current 261 still depend on one ten-year laboratory aggregate (Index 16, Dakar Fann
1981-1990); without it the figure is 205.

The latest positive observation moved from 2015-12-31 to 2012-12-31. Agent 7 retracted the single
unconfirmed 2015 WAHO case (Index 287), which every final source contradicts. The 2012 date comes
from a year-long row of 2 unconfirmed suspected Dakar cases (Index 277). In plainer terms:
- the last dated case is one suspected case in April 2012 (Index 44), and two other zero series
  contradict it;
- the last laboratory-confirmed cases were in 2011 (3 isolates; Bakel cluster, 13-18 Dec 2011).

Weekly modelling input. This was the biggest real gain. Compared with the pre-cycle file
/tmp/SEN_weekly_pre_cycle.csv (git e909991), using the in-memory build of
py/build_weekly_timeseries.py (nothing written):
- Before: the series asserted zero cholera in 11 years in which WHO recorded cases (1971-73,
  1978-79, 1981, 1984, 1988, 2009, 2011, 2012). It also carried 1985-87 at about 720 a year,
  against WHO's 2,988, 476 and 3,150.
- Now: no year with WHO cases is zero. These years are within 8% of WHO: 1971, 1978, 1979,
  1981, 1984, 1986, 1987, 1988, 1995, 2004, 2005, 2006, 2008, 2009 and 2010.
- Still wrong, and to be stated plainly:
  * 1972 is 1.26x WHO.
  * 1973 is 1.55x WHO.
  * 1985 is 1.49x WHO.
  * 1996 is 1.35x WHO.
  * 1997 is 1.19x WHO.
  * 2007 is 1.12x WHO and 2011 is 3.2x WHO. Both are documented choices of Ministry counts
    over WHO's, reviewed below.
  * 1989 is 52 weeks of assumed_zero (confidence 0.5), despite documented presence in
    ./data/SEN/cholera_presence_ai.csv.

Cause of the remaining inflation. It is a builder defect, not bad rows. The builder keeps the
larger per-week value across overlapping same-source national rows, so a legitimate annual total
is added again on top of its own nested weekly rows. The regional West Africa template also peaks
in August-October, which mistimes Magal-driven March-July epidemics such as 1973. The builder
also ignores presence records when it fills assumed zeros. I deleted no legitimate nested rows to
compensate.

Validator. py/validate_quality.py SEN exits 0, with ERROR 0, WARN 34 and INFO 397. At the start
of the audit it was ERROR 0, WARN 37, INFO 396; pre-cycle it was ERROR 0, WARN 20, INFO 4. The
repair tool (py/repair_data_integrity.py --dry-run SEN) reported 0 changes, and there is no
./data/SEN/attribution_review.csv.

covered:

GAPS STILL OPEN, AND WHETHER THE DATA EXIST
-------------------------------------------
1. National 1989. There is presence evidence (Niakhar about 19 cases in April 1989; AFMIC) but no
   count anywhere, and WHO holds no 1989 figure. A count probably exists only in Ministry archives
   in Dakar.
2. 1981. WHO has 428 cases with no place or date, and the WER is silent. Within-year timing is
   likely unrecoverable from public sources.
3. 1985 May-December and 1986-88 within-year and regional detail. Only derived increments,
   Niakhar, and Dakar Sep-Nov 1986 exist. Contemporaneous Ministry and ORSTOM reports exist but are
   not online.
4. 1990-93 and March-December 1997 are inferred absence only, with no explicit zero statement
   found. True absence is plausible on regional and molecular evidence.
5. The places and dates of the 2009-2012 sporadic cases (4, 3, 5 and 1) are unknown, apart from
   Bakel in December 2011.
6. Regional reporting is missing for 2009-2013 and for 13 Jan-31 Dec 2025, when WAHO's cholera
   fields went empty.
7. 29 Sep-4 Oct 2026 is not yet reportable.
8. Leads that exist but could not be reached: the Ministry yearbooks and weekly bulletins (BEH,
   2016-2025, district tables) on informationsanitaire.sec.gouv.sn, which reset the connection;
   and Finger 2016 PNAS Dataset S1 (2005 weekly by arrondissement), behind reCAPTCHA or 403.

2007 VERDICT (Agent 5 replaced WHO weekly values with Ministry values)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Accepted. The five replaced WHO weeks were each below the Ministry's own Diourbel-only count for the
same week; W38-39, for example, were 27 and 11 against 151 and 328. The Ministry-based second wave
sums to 2,038 for 3 Aug-14 Oct, against the Ministry/IFRC cumulative of 2,144. The builder's 2007
total of about 4,468 is 12% above WHO's annual 3,984, but within 2% of the WHO country-office
figure of 4,391 (CCS 2009-2013).
One part I disagree with: derived row 1427 is a WHO-chain residual sitting on Ministry-level weeks,
so ISO weeks 47-52 (about 407 cases) are probably overstated. I down-weighted it to 0.45.
2011 (11 vs 5) uses the Minister's Bakel count, which exceeds WHO's total. That is documented and
accepted.
