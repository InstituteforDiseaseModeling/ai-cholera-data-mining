=== EPIDEMIOLOGICAL FINDINGS ===

Epidemic Wave Structure:
- Wave 1 (2006-2009): Annual escalating outbreaks following CPA peace agreement
  2006: 19,277 cases (CFR 2.9%) - began Yei, spread to Juba and 5+ counties
  2007: 22,412 cases (CFR 1.8%) - Juba 3,157 cases documented separately
  2008: 27,017 cases (CFR 0.57%)
  2009: 48,035 cases (CFR 0.13%) - highest case count of first wave

- Interepidemic period (2010-2013): Confirmed zero-transmission (Documented_Absence)
  Multiple academic sources confirm no cases between 2009 and April 2014

- Wave 2 (2014-2017): Coincides with civil war; three distinct seasonal waves
  2014: 6,421 cases (CFR 2.6%) - 16 counties, 5 states (final December count)
  2015: 1,818 cases (CFR 2.6%) - seasonal wave
  2016-2017: 20,438 cases (CFR 2.1%) - largest wave, 23 counties, 9 states
    Tonj East county alone: 1,451 cases with V. cholerae O1 Inaba confirmed

- Zero period (2018-2022): 3+ years confirmed absence (WHO lab-verified)
  2018-2020: Documented_Absence with 105 negative stool samples in 2019
  2021 through March 2022: Continued absence until Bentiu IDP camp outbreak

- Wave 3 (2022-present): Resurgence linked to IDP camps and Sudan returnees
  2022: 424 cases (Unity/Ruweng, Bentiu IDP focus)
  2023: 1,471 cases (Malakal Upper Nile, Apr-May)
  2024-2026: Explosive ongoing outbreak - 97,516+ cases (largest in country history)
    Origin: Renk border town, Sudan returnees (September 28, 2024)
    Peak affected: 55 counties, 9 states/3 administrative areas by Jan 2026
    Most affected counties: Rubkona 19,557 (20.5%), Juba 11,346 (11.9%)

Geographic Patterns:
- Nile river corridor: Consistent hotspot across all waves (Juba, Malakal, Renk)
- IDP camps: Major amplification sites (Bentiu/Rubkona, Malakal PoC camp)
- Sudan border transmission: Renk corridor (2024 wave origin)
- Seasonal pattern: Outbreaks peak May-November coinciding with rainy season
