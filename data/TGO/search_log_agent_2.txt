AND WHY I DATED IT THE WAY I DID, AFTER GETTING IT WRONG ONCE
My first commit dated these eight districts 2001-02-01..2001-03-01, reasoning from the IHR
de-listing rule that an area listed on 1 March 2001 must have had a case in the preceding
weeks. Then I compared the Togo block across all 47 dated lists and the reasoning collapsed:
THE SET IS IDENTICAL IN EVERY ISSUE FROM 22 FEBRUARY 1996 TO 18 OCTOBER 2001 - checked at
28 separate dates spanning 1996, 1998, 1999, 2000 and 2001 - and the same eight already
appear on 9 January 1992. A list that does not change across nine years is a standing list
WHO never pruned, not a dated snapshot, and it cannot date transmission to February 2001.
I REMOVED the eight rows I had just written and re-committed them over 1996-02-22 to
2001-10-18, the span across which I actually verified the full set, at confidence 0.6, with
the limitation written into the row itself. The apparent variation I first saw in the
1991-1995 lists is an artefact of two things: a line-anchored regex that missed districts
printed mid-line in a five-column layout, and poor OCR on the scanned issues ("Kioto" for
Kloto, "Lpja" for Loja). Both were checked and neither survives.

REUSABLE FINDING: the IRIS "Infected areas as at" series is 250 items covering 1951-2001
and is, for many African countries, the ONLY published sub-national cholera geography of
the 1990s. The working recipe is discover/search/objects?query=%22Infected+areas+as+at%22.
Two traps: pid/find returns empty (use discover with query=handle:"..."), and the district
set must be read as a standing list unless it is shown to change.
