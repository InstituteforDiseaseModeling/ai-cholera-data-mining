WHAT THE NEXT AGENT SHOULD KNOW
  1. Read the WER through the IRIS DSpace API and scan CASE-INSENSITIVELY. The
     infected-area lists print country names in capitals; a case-sensitive
     'Togo' pattern silently misses exactly the entries that date an outbreak's
     start and end. One volume-year is ~50 PDFs and takes about 20 seconds.
  2. The infected-area REMOVAL notices are the single best absence evidence in
     the pre-1990 record - they are an affirmative act by WHO under IHR Article
     7 - but check when the listing STARTED before using it: Togo's Sokode
     entry ran thirteen months past its last notified case.
  3. From 2017 the WER annual cholera article carries an explicit
     "Countries reported 0 cases and deaths in {year}, fulfilling the public
     health surveillance service of 'zero reporting'" list. That, and not
     absence from the case table, is the modern Documented_Absence standard.
  4. WHO AFRO bulletins carry district-level geography in FIGURES that never
     appears in the text layer. Extract the embedded rasters and read them -
     but check the map's scope against the narrative before treating an
     unshaded district as a zero.
