 F2  *** NEW ADJUDICATION EVIDENCE FOR T3 (the 1998/2001 national-series dispute) ***
     Landoh et al's abstract states 12 676 cases and 554 deaths for Togo 1996-2010, drawn
     from "national surveillance data housed in the National Ministry of Health".
     The WHO/JHU annual series for the SAME window sums to 11 913 cases / 552 deaths
     (computed from data/TGO/cholera_data_jhu.csv, rows 12-26).
       -> cases differ by +763, deaths by only +2.
     The two known MoH-vs-WHO deltas are 1998 (3669-3217 = +452) and 2001 (2740-2551 =
     +189), together +641 - i.e. 84% of the entire 763-case discrepancy, with deaths
     essentially unchanged, which is exactly the signature of a case-ascertainment
     difference rather than a different epidemic.
     => The Togolese domestic series is arithmetically consistent with Landoh's MoH total
        in a way the WHO series is not. This is the first quantitative evidence either way.
     COUNTER-INDICATION, recorded so Agent 5 is not misled: Landoh also reports "annual
     national cholera incidence varied from 0.9 to 66 cases per 100 000 population".
     The 0.9 lower bound matches 1997 = 42 cases (the WHO figure) at Togo's 1997
     population almost exactly; and 66/100 000 at Togo's 1998 population (~4.87 M) gives
     ~3 214, i.e. WHO's 3 217, not the MoH's 3 669 (which would be ~75/100 000).
     => STILL UNDETERMINED, but no longer evidence-free. Agent 5 should weigh the total
        (favours MoH) against the incidence range (favours WHO). Resolving it needs
        Landoh's Table 1, which is behind the OUP paywall on all ten routes tried.
