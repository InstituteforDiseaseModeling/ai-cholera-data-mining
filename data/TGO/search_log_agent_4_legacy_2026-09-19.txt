LEAD 3 - RESOLVED. AGENT 1'S MIS-ASSIGNMENT HYPOTHESIS IS FALSIFIED.
  The three entries are exactly as Agent 1 read them, and they are correct:
     WER 1995;70(20)  Togo  1.I-28.II    27 cases   2 deaths
     WER 1995;70(31)  Togo  1.VI-17.VII  55 cases   7 deaths
     WER 1995;70(32)  Togo  18-27.VII    50 cases   7 deaths
  Evidence that the column assignment is right, not merely plausible:
  1. VISUAL. At 240 dpi the WER weekly layout is unambiguous. The country name
     and the notification period share one line; the dotted leader with C and D
     wraps to the line immediately below and belongs to the country named above
     it. The 1994 ANNUAL table prints the same structure on a single line
     ("Togo..... 47  5"), which fixes the convention.
  2. ARITHMETIC FALSIFICATION OF THE ALTERNATIVE. Shifting the numbers up one
     country in WER 1995;70(32) would give Burkina Faso 1 782 cases for
     10.VI-19.VII 1995 against a 1995 ANNUAL total of 1 451. Impossible.
     Shifting down would give Togo 150 for 1.VI-17.VII - worse, not better.
  3. METHOD CONTROL. The identical pipeline reproduces Agent 1's four 1996
     notifications exactly (49/4, 49/7, 6/0, 34/6), and those sum to 138 of a
     146 annual. The pipeline is not biased upward.
  THE DISCREPANCY IS INTERNAL TO WHO, NOT AN ARTEFACT. Togo's contemporaneous
  IHR telegrams total 132 cases / 16 deaths for 1995; Togo's official annual
  return, printed by WHO a year later in WER 1996;71(21), is 65 / 4. Two of the
  three telegrams individually report more deaths than the whole published year.
  WHAT I COMMITTED AND WHY IT IS SAFE.
  Rows 122-124 carry confidence 0.5 (a deliberate downgrade from the Level 1
  floor of 0.9, recorded as such by the writer) and every one of them states
  BOTH figures and "DO NOT SUM" in processing_notes, per the preliminary-versus-
  final rule. They are retained for TIMING, not for magnitude. They cannot
  inflate the modelled series: py/build_weekly_timeseries.py merges per ISO week
  with SOURCE_PRIORITY WHO > JHU > AI and never sums, so the JHU annual row for
  1995 (65/4, Fourier-disaggregated) overwrites every AI cell in 1995.

LEAD 5 - JANUARY 1973 IS NOW LOCALISED, BUT STILL HAS NO COUNT.
  The route was sideways. Bockemuhl & Schroter 1975 (Tropenmed Parasitol), the
  source of the "November 1970 to January 1973" dating, is not open. But its
  companion paper - Bockemuhl & Meinicke, Bull World Health Organ 1976;54(2) -
  IS free, and WHO IRIS mirrors the PMC scan, so my existing IRIS pipeline could
  fetch it where the PMC and Europe PMC PDF endpoints all refused.
  Figure 1 of that paper is a map of the whole 1970-73 epidemic with each focus
  dated in roman-numeral month/year. Read visually at 230 dpi:
     SOKODE   V-VI/72              ANIE    XI/71, and VIII/72 - I/73
     TOMEGBE  VIII/71-IV/72        ADETA   III-V/72
     NUATJA   IV/71-IV/72          LOME/ANECHO coastal endemic zone XI/70-XI/72
  THE ONLY FOCUS STILL ACTIVE IN JANUARY 1973 WAS ANIE, in the Plateaux region.
  The Lome-Anecho littoral had already ended in November 1972. That is a genuinely
  new fact: the tail of Togo's seventh-pandemic wave was inland, not coastal.
  No January 1973 case count exists in this paper, so the month stays open in
  cholera_data_ai.csv and the finding is recorded as presence.
  The same paper also yielded the first sub-national CASE COUNT in the Togolese
  record before 2003: 37 bacteriologically confirmed cases at Nuatja (Notse, Haho
  prefecture) between April 1971 and April 1972 - row 128, entered as cCh.
  And Table 4 dates a northern introduction: Piya, near Lama-Kara (Kozah
  prefecture, Kara region), July 1971, imported by a family travelling from
  Nuatja. That is the northernmost documented point of the 1970-73 epidemic and
  it is 27 years earlier than anything else in the Togolese record for Kara.
