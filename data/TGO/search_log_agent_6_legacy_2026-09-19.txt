METHOD FOR PRIORITY 1 - THE DECISIVE TEST
The question "was the country looking?" is answerable without any cholera
source. If Togo notified OTHER diseases to WHO in 1975-1990 - under IHR(1969)
(cholera, plague, yellow fever), through the EPI/JRF reporting form (measles,
polio, neonatal tetanus, pertussis, diphtheria), or through the dracunculiasis
and meningitis surveillance networks - then Togo had a functioning national
notification channel and its cholera silence is a reported zero. If Togo
notified nothing at all, its cholera silence is a surveillance gap and the
zero rows are asserting something the evidence does not support.

KEY FINDINGS - THE 1974-1990 ERA IS NOT A SURVEILLANCE GAP
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. METHOD FIX THAT MADE THIS POSSIBLE. Agent 3's great contribution was to
   scan the WER CASE-INSENSITIVELY, which recovered the uppercase "TOGO" of the
   infected-area lists. There is a second, independent OCR defect nobody had
   allowed for: the WER's scanned two-column layout SPLITS COUNTRY NAMES
   ACROSS SPACES - "T o g o", "T ogo", "T O G O". A spacing-tolerant pattern
   matches 207 of the 799 issues of 1975-1990 against 147 for the
   case-insensitive pattern. The two Togolese meningitis notifications of 1989
   quoted below are printed as "T o g o , —" and "T ogo (19 May 1989)" and had
   been missed by every previous pass in this workflow. ANY AGENT WORKING A
   SCANNED WER CORPUS SHOULD USE THE SPACING-TOLERANT PATTERN.

2. THE FALSIFICATION TEST CAME BACK CLEAN FOR CHOLERA. Every OCR-split Togo
   hit within +/-30 lines of a cholera mention was read. None is a missed
   Togolese cholera notification in an inferred-absence year. The only numeric
   cholera entries recovered are ones already in the dataset: Togo 6 cases in
   the 1978 annual table (WER 1979;54(17)) and Togo 1 case in the 1988 annual
   table (WER 1989;64(19)). So the absence rows survive the sharper instrument.

3. TOGO APPEARS IN WER DISEASE-NOTIFICATION PRODUCTS IN EVERY SINGLE YEAR
   1975-1990. The notification channel to WHO was continuously live:
     * YAWS - annual returns for 1977, 1978, 1979, 1980, 1981, 1982:
       4 097, 3 936, 2 687, 3 890, 2 584, 3 816 (WER 1984;59(49) Table 1).
       Six consecutive annual national returns inside the absence window.
     * POLIOMYELITIS - unbroken annual series 1975-1985 (WER 1986;61(30)),
       and 1972-1976 with a population denominator in WER 1977;52(51).
     * LOUSE-BORNE TYPHUS 1977 - 235 cases, broken down to the LOME
       SUBDIVISION SANITAIRE, and WHO's own comment that Togo was among
       "countries which have not notified the presence of typhus in recent
       years" (WER 1978;53(33)). That is an affirmative new notification act,
       with sub-national detail, in 1977.
     * MENINGOCOCCAL MENINGITIS - monthly country table entries from the
       1970s; a five-year table 1985-1989 in WER 1990;65(16); and the 1989
       epidemic notifications below.
     * DRACUNCULIASIS - annual national totals throughout the decade,
       aggregated from 19 of Togo's 21 prefectures.
     * YELLOW FEVER - notified under IHR(1969), the SAME quarantinable-disease
       instrument that carries cholera, for 1984 and for 1987.
     * AIDS - dated national returns to WHO Geneva at 15.06.88 (2 cases),
       31.12.88 (15), 22.06.89 (23), 13.12.89 (56) and 01.06.90 (100). The
       date-of-last-report column advances five times in two years.

4. THE THREE VERBATIM STATEMENTS THAT CARRY THE ERA.
   (a) WER 1984;59(42), CDC/WHO report reproduced by permission of the
       Government of Togo: "In 1981 Togo's estimated population was 2.7
       million... The country is divided administratively into 21 prefectures,
       which are grouped into 5 regions. DRACUNCULIASIS IS AN OFFICIALLY
       REPORTABLE DISEASE IN TOGO, BUT PROBABLY ONLY A SMALL PROPORTION OF THE
       TRUE NUMBER OF CASES IS REPORTED. Annual total numbers of cases
       reported over the past decade range from about 1000 to 3 200. The
       disease is endemic throughout Togo, having been reported in 1980 from
       19 of the 21 prefectures."
       -> A legally constituted notifiable-disease system, producing a national
          total EVERY YEAR for the whole decade 1974-1983, fed by 19 of 21
          prefectures. And, in the same sentence, WHO's verdict on its
          sensitivity.
   (b) WER 1986;61(42), Niamey workshop: "The following 8 countries in the
       African Region reported surveillance information covering 1985:
       Burkina Faso, Cameroon, Cote d'Ivoire, Ethiopia, Mali, Mauritania,
       Togo, and Uganda. Although this represents an improvement over recent
       years, several countries are still not providing official reports."
       And: "Although dracunculiasis is officially reportable in at least 8 of
       the countries affected (Benin, Burkina Faso, Cameroon, Cote d'Ivoire,
       Ethiopia, Ghana, Togo, and Uganda), IT IS STILL VASTLY UNDERREPORTED
       EVEN IN THOSE COUNTRIES."
       -> In 1985 Togo was in the MINORITY of African countries that filed.
   (c) WER 1989;64(16), 21 April 1989: "Togo. - The following cases and deaths
       have been declared: 1-31 January 1989, 42 (no deaths); 1-25 February,
       120 (13 deaths); 26 February-11 March, 275 (23 deaths). The total from
       1 January to 25 March 1989 is 558 cases (50 deaths). Mass vaccination is
       under way." WER 1989;64(22), 2 June 1989: "Togo (19 May 1989). - One
       hundred and twenty-eight cases of meningitis (10 deaths) have been
       reported from 26 March to 6 May and the situation seems to have
       improved."
       -> FOUR dated, period-disaggregated epidemic notifications with cases
          AND deaths, filed to WHO Geneva and published within weeks, across
          the first five months of 1989. This is not a country that had stopped
          reporting. It is a country running a live outbreak-notification
          channel - the same WER mechanism a cholera declaration would use.

5. YELLOW FEVER IS THE CLEANEST TEST AND TOGO PASSES IT.
   WER 1990;65(28) Table 1 "Yellow fever: number of cases and deaths notified
   to WHO, 1987-1988" gives TOGO 6 CASES FOR 1987. WER 1985;60(43) gives Togo
   a histopathologically confirmed fatal case diagnosed November 1984 in the
   Centrale Region. Yellow fever is one of the three IHR(1969) quarantinable
   diseases; the notification instrument is identical to cholera's. Togo used
   that instrument in 1984 and in 1987 and did not send a cholera notification
   through it in either year.

6. ONE DOCUMENTED CAPACITY FAILURE, AND IT IS IN SAVANES. WER 1984;59(43), on
   the Ghana Upper East yellow-fever epidemic of Sept-Nov 1984: "The infected
   area around Bawku was close to the Dapango area in the extreme north-west of
   Togo where several suspect fatal cases were observed during the same period
   BUT WHICH IT HAD NOT BEEN POSSIBLE TO INVESTIGATE FOR PURPOSES OF
   CONFIRMATION." Notification capacity and OUTBREAK-INVESTIGATION capacity are
   different things, and in 1984 northern Togo had the first and not the
   second. This is directly relevant to the standing finding that Savanes has
   no cholera case count for any year before 2020: the region's problem was
   never that nobody reported, it was that nobody could go and confirm.

7. WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT SETTLE - THE CENTRAL CONCLUSION OF THIS BATCH.
   The 1975-1990 rows are NOT surveillance gaps. "Nobody was looking" is false
   and can now be shown false year by year. But the uncertainty is RELOCATED,
   not removed. Three things sit against a naive upgrade:
     * WHO's own verdict on this same system: "only a small proportion of the
       true number of cases is reported", "vastly underreported".
     * WHO's own verdict on the cholera table specifically: "Evidence was again
       received of the presence of cholera in a number of other countries which
       failed to declare it" (WER 1991;66(19), "Cholera in 1990").
     * 1990 - the ONLY year of the run with an independent Togolese national
       source - is the year the inference FAILS. The MoH SIMR series plots a
       clearly non-zero 1990 bar; WER 1991;66(19) records that cholera
       "reappeared in Ghana, which had not reported an outbreak since 1986",
       Ghana being the neighbour whose border runs along greater Lome; and WHO
       recorded nothing at all for Togo. Agent 5 retracted the 1990 zero for
       exactly this reason.
   So the residual risk on these rows is CHOLERA-SPECIFIC NON-DECLARATION BY A
   FUNCTIONING SYSTEM, not blindness - and Togo is demonstrated to have done
   precisely that at least once, in 1990. Confidence was therefore raised ONLY
   where the evidence goes beyond the general live-system assumption Agent 3
   had already priced in, i.e. where Togo used the CHOLERA-CARRYING channel
   (1987, yellow fever) or filed dated epidemic notifications (1989), or where
   Togo is individually named as a filer (1985). 1983 and 1984 were HELD at
   0.55 despite strong capacity evidence, because a neighbour reported cholera
   in both years and the 1990 precedent shows what Togo does in that situation.

1. THE 1990s CRISIS WAS NOT A SURVEILLANCE COLLAPSE, AND TWO DATED INSTRUMENTS
   PROVE IT. The political collapse is real: general strike from November 1992
   that "largely shut down Lome for months"; 25 January 1993 shooting of
   demonstrators; more than 300 000 Togolese displaced to Benin, Ghana and the
   interior; EU development cooperation suspended from OCTOBER 1992 and not
   re-established until 2007, a fourteen-year hiatus. But:
     * TOGO'S AIDS DATE-OF-LAST-REPORT TO WHO GENEVA NEVER STALLS. Read off the
       WER country tables issue by issue: 01.06.90 (100 cases), 31.12.91
       (1 278), 03.04.92 (1 278), 02.03.93 (1 953), 10.12.93 (2 381), 23.02.94
       (3 472), 08.06.95 (5 109), 30.06.95 (5 609), 13.05.96 (6 466), 30.09.96
       (7 256), 14.03.97 (7 993), 31.03.98 (9 204), 08.03.99 (10 827). TWO
       dated national returns were filed in 1993 itself.
     * WER 1994;69(17) PRINTS TOGO'S DRACUNCULIASIS COUNT FOR EVERY MONTH OF
       1993 - 1242, 1374, 842, 657, 472, 482, 752, 715, 941, 1124, 995, 798,
       annual 10 394 - and WHO's narrative says: "Togo: As shown by the
       indices, this programme continued to make good progress DESPITE
       CONSIDERABLE POLITICAL TURMOIL, as a result of energetic action by
       national coordinator and assistance provided by WHO, UNICEF, the United
       States Peace Corps and Global 2000."
   CAVEAT RECORDED IN THE NOTES: guinea worm was a VERTICAL donor-staffed
   programme and WHO credits four external agencies and one individual, not
   the health system. The AIDS return, which came from the Ministry itself, is
   the stronger instrument of the two.

2. 1993 ADJUDICATED AS FAR AS IT CAN BE. Agent 5 called WHO's 19 against the
   SIMR archive's ~269 the worst unresolved year. Surveillance context does
   not break the tie but it REMOVES THE MOST ATTRACTIVE EXPLANATION: 19 is a
   genuine transmitted figure from a channel that was demonstrably working in
   March and December 1993. What remains are three non-decisive reasons to
   think it too low - Ghana notified 1 448 cases / 38 deaths and Cote d'Ivoire
   724 in 1993 (WER 1994;69(28) Table 1) while Togo reported 19; over 300 000
   Togolese were displaced INTO Ghana and Benin in the first months of 1993 and
   any cholera among them would be counted in the receiving country's return,
   not Togo's (Benin notified 10/2); and WHO states evidence was received of
   cholera in countries which failed to declare it. Against ~269 stands Agent
   5's demonstration that the same chart errs in BOTH directions before 1994.
   VERDICT: irreconcilable on counts, NOT a surveillance gap. No new count and
   no zero written. Recorded on row 170.

3. THE TOGOLESE ARCHIVE IS NOW DATED, FROM THE SOURCE FOUR AGENTS COULD NOT
   READ. The Landoh et al 2013 full text is paywalled and Agents 1, 2, 4 and 5
   each failed to obtain it. The ABSTRACT, retrieved through the PubMed MCP
   (PMID 24101639), carries the methods sentence that matters: "We reviewed
   national surveillance data housed in the National Ministry of Health.
   Districts submitted reports of summary weekly case counts and deaths at the
   national level. DATA WERE AVAILABLE AT THE DISTRICT LEVEL DURING 2008-2010
   AND AT THE NATIONAL LEVEL FROM 1996 ONWARD. Microbiological confirmation
   usually was not performed, and case identification was based on clinical
   suspicion." So:
     - Ministry-held NATIONAL cholera series begins 1996.
     - Ministry-held DISTRICT-level series begins 2008.
     - Everything before 1996 in this dataset necessarily comes from the WER,
       which is exactly what Agents 3 and 4 found by trial and error.
     - All of 1996-2010 is CLINICALLY SUSPECTED, never confirmed: sCh only.
   AND THE TRANSITION HAS A CAUSE. District-level retention starts in 2008, the
   year after EU development cooperation resumed in 2007 from a suspension
   running since October 1992. The rebuild of sub-national record-keeping and
   the return of development financing are one year apart.

4. THE HEALTH-SYSTEM TRAJECTORY IS QUANTIFIED. Same paper: the national cholera
   case-fatality ratio was 12-17% during 1996-1997, falling to <1% during
   2008-2010. A 12-17% CFR is not a surveillance statistic - it says a
   suspected cholera patient in mid-1990s Togo had little access to
   rehydration. That is what a decade without donor health financing looks
   like, and it independently corroborates the picture from finding 1: a
   REPORTING system that kept working attached to a TREATMENT system that did
   not. Note for Agent 7: the two CFR_high WARNs on rows 34 (16.7%) and 39
   (17.6%), both 1996-1999, are not extraction errors - they sit exactly on
   Landoh's independently published 12-17% national CFR for that period.

5. 2017 SOLVED. Togo's absence from the WER 2017 zero-reporting list is a
   WHO-QUESTIONNAIRE ARTEFACT, not a Togolese surveillance failure.
     - The 2017 list, read verbatim from the cached WER 2018;93(38), names 37
       countries and Togo is not among them.
     - Togo IS in the 2018 and 2019 lists.
     - List membership fluctuates in a way that tracks questionnaire returns,
       not capacity: Ghana is in the 2017 list and not the 2018 one; Benin,
       Burkina Faso and Chad are in the 2018 list and not the 2017 one. None
       of them lost and regained a surveillance system in twelve months.
     - Decisively, Agent 5's recovery of the Ministry's five-year DSIUSR table
       shows Togo's own system DID produce a 2017 figure: 0/0 suspected and
       confirmed. The data existed; only the annual return to Geneva is
       missing. Row 105 therefore stays at 0.95 with surveillance=operational.

6. IDSR/SIMR ADOPTION DATE - NOT FOUND, AND HONESTLY SO. WHO AFRO adopted the
