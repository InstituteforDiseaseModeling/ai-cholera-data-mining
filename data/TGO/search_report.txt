EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
================================================================================
Togo is now the best-covered country in the MOSAIC framework on the headline
metric, and that headline metric is the least interesting thing about it.

Effective coverage rose from 72.7% to 99.9% (495 -> 680 of 681 months), gap
periods from 5 to 1, and staleness from 545 days to 24. Sixteen gap-years with
six or more missing months became zero. The AI layer contributed 609 covered
months, 185 of them as the only source.

HERE IS THE PART THAT MATTERS: 172 of those 185 AI-only months (93%) are
covered ONLY by a zero / absence row. Twelve have a real dated observation with
counts; one is covered only by a multi-year aggregate. All fourteen of the
closed gap-years - 1973, 1975-76, 1979-87, 1989-90 - were closed with zeros, not
with counts, and twelve of those zeros are Inferred_Absence at confidence
0.55-0.80, meaning they are inferences from WHO's silence rather than from
anyone stating that Togo had no cholera. "99.9% coverage" means every month is
accounted for. It does not mean every month was counted. A reader who takes
99.9% at face value will over-trust the 1975-1990 segment of this series.

What the run genuinely added, beyond absence assertions:
  * A sub-annual decomposition of the 1996 and 1999 epidemics from the WHO
    Weekly Epidemiological Record that reconciles EXACTLY to the JHU annual
    totals (1999: 667 cases / 31 deaths to the unit; 1996: 17 deaths to the
    unit, 138 of 146 cases).
  * The first sub-national data Togo has ever had for most of its record: 174
    of 273 rows are sub-national, across 38 distinct administrative units,
    against a JHU baseline whose only non-zero sub-national rows for Togo are
    six, in 2013 and 2016.
  * The 1970-74 El Tor epidemic at weekly, district resolution from WER
    notifications - which disproves the standing belief, sourced to Landoh et al
    2013, that Togolese district-level data begins in 2008.
  * A monthly reconstruction of the 2024-25 epidemic (rows 202-206) summing to
    exactly the documented national total of 281.
  * 170 of 192 sources at Level 1 reliability; only 5 at Level 3 and none at
    Level 4.

Four specific defects were referred to this audit. Three were real and are
repaired; the fourth was mis-described in the briefing and the real version of
it was cleared on arithmetic. A fifth and larger problem, which nobody referred,
was found and quantified: a systemic defect in the weekly time-series builder
that inflates Togo's 2024 national series by 54% and affects 26 of the 40
countries. It is NOT fixed here, deliberately.

WHAT WAS FOUND AND WHAT WAS DONE
================================================================================

1. LOME / GOLFE LABEL SPLIT  -> REPAIRED
   Golfe and Agoe-Nyive turned out NOT to be defective: every pre-2020 row uses
   the Maritime parent and every post-2020 row uses Grand Lome, without a single
   exception. That is correct, because those prefectures genuinely transferred
   region in 2020.
   Lome WAS defective, and the split was inside one era, not across the reform:
   twelve 1970-74 rows said Maritime::Lome while thirteen other pre-2020 rows
   said Grand Lome. The harm is documented in the file itself - tombstones
   274/275/276 are Agent 6 re-adding rows 131/132/133 under the other label,
   with the retraction reason "the duplicate guard did not fire because the two
   rows use different location strings for the same place".

   CONVENTION NOW IN FORCE, STATED ONCE FOR DOWNSTREAM AGGREGATION:
     (a) The Lome urban core is ONE stable patch in every era:
         AFR::TGO::Grand Lome, with its five arrondissements at
         AFR::TGO::Grand Lome::Lome D1..D5.
     (b) Golfe and Agoe-Nyive follow the region in force at the observation
         date: AFR::TGO::Maritime::Golfe before 2020, AFR::TGO::Grand Lome::Golfe
         from 2020. THEY ARE ONE PLACE - concatenate the two labels for a
         per-prefecture series; never sum them.
     (c) AFR::TGO::Maritime and its entire subtree NEVER contain Lome, in any
         era. Verified against the source quotes on all 9 bare-Maritime rows:
         the Togolese Ministry tabulates Lome-Commune as a reporting region peer
         of Maritime, not inside it.
     (d) Bare AFR::TGO::Maritime DOES include Golfe and Agoe-Nyive before 2020
         and DOES NOT from 2020. It therefore denotes a smaller territory in
         rows 59/65/86 than in rows 77/96/165/190/236/269. This is a real
         administrative fact and cannot be engineered away; it is now stated on
         every one of those rows.
     (e) CROSS-LAYER: JHU nests Lome UNDER Maritime. AFR::TGO::Maritime does not
         mean the same territory in the AI and JHU layers. Map JHU
         Maritime::Lome -> AI Grand Lome before any cross-layer join. Present
         exposure is small: JHU's only non-zero Togo sub-national rows are six,
         in 2013 and 2016.

   Actions: 12 rows relabelled; 9 bare-Maritime rows given an explicit
   territory token; 17 Golfe/Agoe rows given a two-parent crosswalk token. All
   through py/revise_observation.py. A label-insensitive duplicate scan after
   the repair finds zero duplicates.

2. SUPERSEDED METADATA STILL MARKED Active  -> REPAIRED
   Metadata 176-184 - nine IRIS handles Agent 6 fabricated by guesswork - were
   Status='Active' with the warning buried in Description. Set to 'Superseded';
   zero live rows cite them, confirmed after the change.
   I verified both sides myself rather than trusting the briefing. The retired
   handles return HTTP 200 pointing at unrelated documents ("Influenza =
   Grippe", "TYPHUS EXANTHEMATIQUE", "THE WHO AIDS SERIES", and WER 1996;71(38)
   where WER 1977;52(21) was claimed). The seven replacements plus 168 all
   return HTTP 200 with titles matching the cited issue exactly.
   GENERALISABLE LESSON FOR THE WHOLE PIPELINE: a fabricated IRIS handle is
   LIVE. py/check_urls.py and any liveness-based check cannot detect this defect
   class. Only resolving the title can.
   py/revise_observation.py had no way to change a Status, so the only route was
   hand-editing. I added --set-status, which refuses if any live row still cites
   the entry - so a bad citation cannot be laundered into a clean-looking
   "Superseded" record.

3. multiyear_aggregate WARNs (39 at audit entry, 40 at exit)  -> JUSTIFIED, NOT SILENCED
   All checked individually, none sampled. Every one is capped at confidence 0.7,
   every one carries explicit period-total language, and every one carries an
   explicit non-additivity phrase. The five national ones cross-check against JHU: 2007-2013 is exact to
   the unit (983 = 983), 2000-2006 +2.0%, 2004-2016 +1.7%, 1996-2010 +6.4%.
   Thirty-four of the original 39 are sub-national and never reach the national
   weekly series.
   These WARNs are "verify this" flags that have now been verified. They should
   remain. No threshold was edited.

4. CFR_high WARNs  -> THE BRIEFING NAMED THE WRONG ROWS
   The two WARNs are Index 42 and Index 47, not Index 243 and Index 103. The
   validator sets CFR_SMALL_DENOM = 25 and skips the CFR check below 25 cases,
   so the two rows the briefing named are not flagged and never were.
     Index 42 (1999, 9/54 = 16.7%): AI rows 39+40+41+42+171 sum to exactly the
       JHU 1999 annual of 667 cases and 31 deaths. Real tail-of-epidemic ratio.
     Index 47 (1996, 6/34 = 17.6%): deaths sum exactly to the JHU annual 17;
       Landoh et al report a Togolese national CFR of 12-17% for 1996-97.
   Both already at the 0.7 cap. Cleared.
   Assessed anyway: Index 243 (Lacs 2014, 5 cases / 2 deaths) is a
   small-denominator artefact quoted verbatim by the source - correct data, but
   a CFR on n=5 carries no information. Index 103 (Agoe-Nyive 2024, 4/11) is a
   GENUINE denominator mismatch, already documented as such: the denominator is
   laboratory-confirmed cases only while the numerator includes community
   deaths. Its counts are usable; its CFR field is not.

5. SYSTEMIC WEEKLY-BUILDER DEFECT  -> QUANTIFIED, DELIBERATELY NOT FIXED
   py/build_weekly_timeseries.py evaluates its aggregate-suppression test per
   source, so an AI period row is never suppressed by WHO or JHU weekly coverage
   of the same weeks - it is overlaid and ADDS.
   Togo 2024 traced end to end: AI rows 202-206 sum to exactly 281, the
   documented national total. The weekly series nevertheless renders 432.9
   (WHO observed 282.0 + JHU 28.0 + AI 122.9) - 54% high, and neither the WHO
   series nor the AI series but a mixture.
   Togo years affected: 2013 +93%, 2020 +103%, 2021 +93%, 2024 +54%, 2015 +43%,
   2002 +37%, 2014 +31%.
   NOT TGO-SPECIFIC: across all 40 countries, 115 of 452 country-years with a
   JHU annual above 20 cases (25%) show a weekly sum more than 25% above that
   annual, in 26 of 40 countries. Togo is among the milder cases; the worst are
   GNB 2006 (21531 vs 37) and KEN 2014 (6292 vs 35).
   Rows 202-206 were NOT retracted to make the number look right. They are
   correct and carry within-year timing the WHO dashboard lacks. The fix belongs
   in the builder, changes all 40 countries, and needs a human decision.

GAPS STILL OPEN, AND WHETHER THE DATA EXISTS AT ALL
================================================================================
  * 2026-09-01..2026-09-19 (19d). Publication lag only. No WHO AFRO, WHO HQ or
    ECDC product covering September 2026 existed on the collection date. Closes
    itself. DO NOT BACK-FILL.
  * 1975-1990 remains the weakest segment of the record, and it is weak in a way
    the coverage number hides. Twelve of these fourteen years are Inferred
    Absence - inferred from Togo's non-appearance in WER annual tables, by a
    country that was simultaneously filing yellow-fever returns through the same
    IHR channel. DATA PROBABLY DOES NOT EXIST in the international record. The
    Togolese national archive is the only remaining route.
  * 1993 is irreconcilable and open: WHO reports 19 cases, the Togolese archive
    ~269-274, and Agent 6 showed the reporting channel was working through the
    1993 political crisis. Two stable series from a working system differing
    14-fold. NOT resolvable from public sources.
  * 1990-1993 and 1998: which Togolese national series is correct is
    UNDETERMINED. WHO currently wins by builder load order, not by adjudication.
  * Savanes region pre-2020 magnitude is unknown. Presence is established for
    1998 and 2001 but no count exists anywhere in the public record. Djadou et
    al, Med Afr Noire 2001;48(5):187-190, on the 1998 Dapaong epidemic, defeated
    five agents. Interlibrary loan is the only route left. DATA EXISTS, offline.
  * 1991-2005 sub-national counts do not exist publicly. WER "infected areas"
    lists name eight districts with no numbers; these are recorded in
    ./data/TGO/cholera_presence_ai.csv, correctly, rather than as false zeros.
  * 2024-25 outbreak GEOGRAPHY is still mostly unknown. 282 national cases for
    2024 and 164 for 2025 with almost no sub-national attribution. The two
    documents that would settle it - the Annuaire des Statistiques Sanitaires
    2024 and the Rapport annuel de performance 2024 - are respectively
    unpublished and archived as an HTML shell. DATA EXISTS BUT IS NOT YET
    PUBLISHED. Retry in a few months; this gap closes itself.
  * Landoh et al 2013 (doi 10.1093/infdis/jit244) is genuinely paywalled -
    Unpaywall is_oa=false, no PMC deposit. It holds the 2005-2010 regional
    breakdown. Its figures are visible in search snippets and sum correctly, but
    they were NOT committed because they could not be read in the source. That
    was the right call.
  * The UNICEF Cholera Platform hotspot dossiers are PROVEN unrecoverable:
    Wayback stored 1,048,576 of 3,021,383 bytes and range requests past that
    offset 404. Spend no further budget.
  * MoH statistical yearbooks for 2015, 2017 and 2018 are on neither the
    ministry server nor Wayback. The 2017 edition is on Scribd.

  THE EPISTEMIC BOUND ON EVERY ZERO ROW, stated because MOSAIC consumes these
  zeros as data: a zero means NO CASES WERE ASCERTAINED AND REPORTED. It does
  not mean V. cholerae was absent. Opintan et al 2021 find West African lineage
  persistence "in the absence of reported cholera cases", with Togolese isolates
  among the closest relatives of Ghanaian ones.
