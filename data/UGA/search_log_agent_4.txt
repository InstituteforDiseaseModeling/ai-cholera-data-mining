METHOD NOTE - WHY THIS AGENT CAN GET WHAT AGENT 3 COULD NOT
Agent 3 scanned the WER weekly "Diseases subject to the Regulations" archive by
downloading the .txt bitstream that IRIS serves alongside each issue. That text
is a raw OCR dump in which the WER's two-column page layout is interleaved line
by line, so country names and the numbers on the same printed line belong to
DIFFERENT countries. Agent 3 correctly refused to extract numbers from it
(finding F31) and took only the unambiguous district names.
This agent downloads the PDF bitstream instead and runs `pdftotext -layout`,
which reconstructs the column geometry. The handle lists Agent 3 built are
cached in /tmp/wk{year}/ and are reused, so re-enumeration costs no queries.

G1. THE METHOD WORKS. Agent 3's finding F31 - "the 1979, 1980, 1991 and 1994

G3. EPIDEMIC SHAPE, PREVIOUSLY INVISIBLE. The 1978-80 epidemic was not three
    separate annual events. It began in the week of 14 November 1978, peaked in
    the last week of December 1978 (743 cases), decayed through January and
    February 1979, went unreported from mid-February to late December 1979,
    resurged in the week of 13 January 1980 (682 cases), and ended after a
    second April-May 1980 wave. The 1980 wave is biphasic with a reporting
    silence from late January to mid-April. Before this batch the AI and JHU
    layers held only three annual totals for this entire epidemic.

G9.  AGENT 3'S 1981 CONCLUSION IS INDEPENDENTLY CONFIRMED. I re-audited all 12

     rests on two independent extractions rather than one.

G10. A TRAP IN THESE LISTS, AND A CORRECTION FOR FUTURE AGENTS.
     The WER infected-areas list is printed in four to six columns and is
     ordered BY DISEASE - Plague, then Yellow fever, then Cholera - with a
     country block under each. Assigning a country block to the nearest disease
     heading ABOVE IT BY LINE NUMBER is wrong, because a heading in column 1
     can sit below, in line terms, the entries it governs in column 4. Under
     the naive method Uganda's "Western Region / Nebbi District" entry reads as
     a CHOLERA listing throughout 1993-1996. It is not: in column reading order
     it lands squarely in the PLAGUE section, which is correct - Nebbi is the
     West Nile plague focus and Uganda notified PLAGUE for Nebbi in 1986, 1989
     and 1993 (Agent 3's finding F26). No Nebbi cholera row was created.

G11. NEW SUB-NATIONAL GEOGRAPHY FOR 1992-1996: KASESE DISTRICT.
     In column reading order, Uganda's entry in the CHOLERA section of the
     monthly infected-areas list is "Kasese District", and it is there
     continuously from the list as at 1 October 1992 to the list as at
     28 November 1996 - verified in 19 separate issues across five years.
     Agent 2 recorded "1972-1996 district-level geography: no sub-national data
     exists in any layer for any year of this period"; Agent 3 closed 1979-1983;
     this closes 1992-1996. Recorded as five presence rows, because the WER
     infected-areas list carries names and never counts.
     Kasese is on Lakes George and Edward and is one of the country profile's
     named cholera hotspots, so the attribution is epidemiologically coherent.

G12. A SECOND ZERO ROW IS CONTRADICTED: 1993 (row 65).
     Uganda filed no cholera return for 1993 - confirmed by direct GHO API pull
     and by Uganda's absence from the WER annual cholera articles - and the AI
     layer asserts a national zero for the year. But WHO carried Kasese
     District on its CHOLERA infected-areas list in at least eight monthly
     lists from 11 March to 18 November 1993. This is the same class of
     evidence, and the same caveat, as 1981: under IHR (1969) an area is
     removed only on the country's notification that transmission has ceased,
     so a long listing may partly reflect unnotified clearance. It is weaker
     than the 1981 case, because Uganda's last notification before it (5072
     cases to 15 September 1992) is closer in time, so staleness is a live
     alternative. It is still incompatible with a CONFIDENT zero.
     ACTION FOR AGENT 7: demote row 65 (1993 national zero) - at minimum lower
     its confidence and change its evidence label to Inferred_Absence; consider
     retirement alongside rows 57 and 60. Note that this REVERSES Agent 3's
     "1993 national zero CONFIRMED and strengthened" verdict, which rested on
     Uganda's June 1993 IHR PLAGUE notification proving the notification
     channel was open. That argument is sound but cuts the other way once the
     cholera infected-areas listing is read correctly: the channel was open,
     Uganda used it for plague, and WHO still had a Ugandan district on the
     cholera list all year.

G20. THE 1985 "541 CASES" PAPER IS NOT UGANDA. A promising lead - Djeddah et
     al., "An outbreak of cholera in a refugee camp in Africa", Eur J Epidemiol

     of 541 cases of cholera ... between May 7 and July 19, 1985 among the
     9,929 displaced persons present in a refugee camp in Africa ... only 12

     is "Africa". Two contemporaneous papers place the 1985 refugee-camp
     cholera epidemics of that exact window in EASTERN Sudan - Mulholland,
     "Cholera in Sudan: an account of an epidemic in a refugee camp in eastern
     Sudan, May-June 1985", Disasters 1985;9(4):247-58 (1,175 cases, 13
     inpatient and 38 home deaths), and Sorensen & Dissler, "Practical
     experience with the management of a cholera outbreak in a refugee camp in
     eastern Sudan, 1985", Disasters 1988;12(3):274-81 (Wad Sherife camp, 1,793
     admissions, CFR 1.8%) - and those camps held Ethiopian and Eritrean
     famine refugees in Kassala, 1,500 km from Uganda. NOT ATTRIBUTED TO
     UGANDA. No row created. Recorded so the next agent does not re-open it.

G24. AND WHY THAT AUDIT DOES NOT LET ME UPGRADE THE 1984-1990 ZEROS - THE
     HONEST LIMIT OF THIS EVIDENCE. It is tempting to read "Uganda was on no
     cholera infected-area list in 1984, 1985, 1988, 1990" as positive evidence
     of absence, which would contradict Agent 3's recommendation to RETIRE row
     60 (1985). 1987 forbids that inference. In 1987 Uganda notified 140 cases
     and 50 deaths for 21.XII.1986-7.III.1987 (row 137, WER 62(20):148) - a real
     epidemic with a 36% case-fatality rate - and in that same year ELEVEN
     infected-area lists name Uganda and every single one of them is PLAGUE.
     Cholera occurred and no Ugandan area was ever added to the cholera list.
     The infected-areas list is therefore ASYMMETRIC evidence: presence on it
     is strong evidence that WHO regarded the country as infected, absence from
     it is weak evidence of anything, because it depends on the country
     notifying an area in the first place. Agent 3's recommendation on row 60
     stands unaltered; I have neither strengthened nor weakened it, and I have
     not created any zero row of my own for 1984-1990.

G26. HOW THE TWO HALF-DATED 1999 WINDOWS WERE HANDLED. WER No.25 prints
     "18.IV-..." with 1275/55 and WER No.28 prints "...-19.VI" with 1138/58,
     each with one endpoint replaced by WHO's "data not yet received" marker.
     Character-position analysis settles which endpoint is which: in No.28 the
     cell "19.VI" occupies columns 61-65, aligned with the END-date position of
     the Madagascar row above ("29.VI-5.VII", start 56-60, end 62-66). The two
     are therefore consecutive segments of 18 April to 19 June 1999 with an
     unprinted internal boundary. Rather than invent that boundary date, or
     drop 2413 cases, they are recorded as ONE row for 18 April - 19 June with
     both counts summed and both original figures preserved in the quote, at
     confidence 0.7. The alternative reading - that No.28 revises No.25 - was
     rejected because every other 1999 window begins the day after the previous
     ends (27.I/28.I, 5.II/6.II, 17.IV/18.IV, 19.VI/20.VI), and because the
     volume index lists six separate page references, one per issue.
