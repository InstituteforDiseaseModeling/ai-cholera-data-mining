THE INHERITED EVIDENTIAL PROBLEM, STATED PLAINLY
All ten surviving zero rows rest on ONE inference: "Uganda is not named in the WER
annual cholera notification table for year Y, therefore Uganda had no cholera in
year Y." Agent 4's finding G24 already demonstrated the weak direction of that
inference for the monthly infected-areas list (1987: a 140-case, 50-death Ugandan
epidemic ran while Uganda appeared on ZERO cholera infected-area lists). The annual
table is a different instrument from the monthly list and is not disposed of by
G24 - but it is the same kind of instrument: it records NOTIFICATION, not DISEASE.
Absence from it is a fact about Uganda's relationship with WHO, not about Vibrio
cholerae. My job is to find the independent evidence that decides, year by year,
whether that relationship was working.

THE DISCRIMINATOR I WILL USE
Not "did Uganda file anything with WHO" (Agent 3's test - smallpox and plague
returns) but "was Uganda's CHOLERA case-finding sensitive enough that a zero
means zero". Two directly measurable proxies:
  (a) SENSITIVITY AT THE BOTTOM - does Uganda notify SMALL cholera events in the
      adjacent years? Notifying 1 imported case proves the channel detects at n=1.
      Notifying only a 36%-CFR mass-casualty event proves it detects only at the
      catastrophic tail, and a zero from such a system is uninformative.
  (b) REGIONAL PRESSURE - how many of the five neighbours (COD, KEN, RWA, SSD/SDN,
      TZA) were notifying cholera in the same year. A zero surrounded by silence is
      cheap; a zero surrounded by five infected neighbours must be paid for.

      Uganda had one of Africa's best health systems in the 1960s; the Amin
      decade produced "a collapse of the country and an exodus of doctors and
      other professions"; the 1979 liberation war and subsequent instability
      "further aggravated the poor health services"; from December 1980 the
      health budget fell from ~7.5% to 3.5% of government spending.
    - Macrae, Zwi & Birungi, "A Healthy Peace? Post-conflict rehabilitation of
      the health sector in Uganda 1986-1992" (LSHTM/Makerere) covers exactly the

      rather than alleviated, the health crisis inherited in 1986", with
      "uncoordinated interventions of donors" and a "fragmented project
      approach" delaying a comprehensive policy.

      diarrhoeal diseases, nutrition surveillance in Karamoja and an essential
      drugs programme" - not with communicable-disease notification.

      literature; there is no source placing a functioning national HMIS before
      the mid-1980s and the national roll-out is consistently placed in
      1996-1997. IDSR "was adopted for use in Uganda in 2000" (Pan African
      Medical Journal, Uganda surveillance assessment), and the first systematic
      performance review covers 2001-2007.

    instrument in Uganda capable of supporting a national zero. For 1974-1977
    there was a functioning WHO annual return, but still no HMIS - which is why
    those rows should stay at "partial", not be upgraded to "operational".

WHAT RETIRING FIVE ROWS COSTS AND WHY IT IS WORTH IT
It removes five asserted disease-free years from the 1980s. That is a real loss
of apparent coverage and I am not pretending otherwise. But those five years were
never observations; they were the absence of an observation, re-encoded as a
measurement of zero. The asymmetry is decisive: an empty period contributes
nothing to a likelihood, whereas a false zero during a period when 2-4 of five
neighbours were epidemic contributes a hard, wrong negative, and is
indistinguishable downstream from a real one. Uganda's cholera may genuinely have
been absent in some of these years - the episodic pattern makes it plausible, and
no positive evidence of 1984-1990 Ugandan cholera exists (H14). But "plausibly
absent" and "measured as zero" are different claims, and only one of them belongs
in cholera_data_ai.csv.
