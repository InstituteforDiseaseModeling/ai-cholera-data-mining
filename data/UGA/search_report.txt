1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
================================================================================

The 7-agent AI-driven cholera surveillance data enhancement workflow for Uganda
successfully augmented the integrated JHU/WHO baseline (which had 69.9% temporal
coverage with 201 of 668 months missing) by systematically searching, validating,
and integrating peer-reviewed academic literature, WHO AFRO bulletins, Uganda
National Institute of Public Health (UNIPH) outbreak investigations, and UN
humanitarian situation reports. The workflow produced 46 quantitative cholera
observations and 30 validated source records spanning 1970-2025.

The enhancement is dominated by HIGH-QUALITY Level 1 evidence: 93.3% of sources
are peer-reviewed academic papers, WHO publications, or Uganda government
health ministry reports, with a mean confidence weight of 0.917 (median 0.95).
Critical contributions include (1) a documented 1970 pre-cholera absence
record (the seventh pandemic reached Uganda in 1971); (2) full filling of the
2021 baseline gap with 173 cases (Nakivale Refugee Settlement and national
totals); (3) partial filling of the 2024-2025 current gap with 152 cases
across 2 outbreaks (Elegu border January 2024, national January-March 2025);
and (4) 33 new sub-national administrative-level outbreak observations
enriching baseline national data with district granularity.

A key methodological finding from Agent 3 (Zero-Transmission Validator) is
that the six historical gap periods between 1972 and 1993 should be classified
as SURVEILLANCE GAPS (not zero-transmission), based on peer-reviewed evidence
(Wamala et al. 2013, PLOS NTD) that Uganda "reported cholera every 2-4 years"
during this period — i.e., the gaps reflect reporting failures during periods
of political instability (Amin, Obote II, NRA bush war), not disease absence.
This re-classification prevents inappropriate zero-coding of years when
cholera was likely present but unreported.

7. REMAINING LIMITATIONS
================================================================================

Data Limitations:
  1. Six historical gap periods (1972-1974, 1976-1977, 1981, 1983-1986,
     1988-1990, 1993) remain unfilled. Per Wamala et al. 2013 these are
     SURVEILLANCE failures, not disease absence. Filling requires offline
     WHO WER archives, Uganda MOH archives, or missionary/colonial records.
  2. April-August 2025 currently unfilled; will resolve as WHO AFRO publishes
     additional monthly bulletins.
  3. Death counts unavailable for 7/46 observations (sCh-only). These mostly
     correspond to annual aggregate totals from secondary studies that did
     not separately report mortality.
  4. CFR computable for 41/46 observations (89.1%).
  5. Confirmed-case counts (cCh) available for only 10/46 observations (21.7%)
     — typical for surveillance literature emphasizing suspected counts.

Validation Limitations:
  1. CFR > 15% in Row 45 (Butabika 2008 nosocomial, 27.78%) is contextually
     justified but represents an unusual setting; users should be aware
     this entry should not be used to estimate population-level CFR.
  2. Multi-year aggregate CFR (Row 33: 2007-2011 = 2.38%) is necessarily
     approximate; confidence_weight 0.80 reflects this.

Methodology Limitations:
  1. Web-based collection cannot access offline archives needed for
     1970s-1990s gap-filling.
  2. Peer-reviewed literature lags real-time surveillance by 1-3 years,
     limiting documentation of 2025+ outbreaks until publication catches up.
  3. Local-language (Luganda/Acholi/Lugbara) news sources not systematically
     searched (English-language Uganda surveillance is generally comprehensive,
     but specialized local outbreak reports may be missed).

Future Priorities:
  1. Manual access to Uganda Ministry of Health archives (Kampala) for
     1970s-1990s outbreak records.
  2. WHO Weekly Epidemiological Record physical archive access for
     1970-1993 Uganda-specific reports.
  3. Monthly automated WHO AFRO bulletin monitoring for 2025+ data.
  4. UNIPH district outbreak investigation page monitoring for new reports.
