1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY - READ THIS BEFORE THE COVERAGE NUMBER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Agents 1-6 added 154 rows and took Uganda's effective coverage from 72.7% to
99.9% of the 681 months from January 1970 to September 2026. That 99.9% was
wrong. Eight of the added rows asserted a national sCh=0 for a whole calendar
year on the strength of Uganda's non-appearance from a WHO annual notification
table - and five of those eight tables say, in their own words, that cholera
occurred in countries which failed to declare it. Two more years are directly
contradicted: WHO itself carried Ugandan districts on its cholera infected-areas
list throughout 1981 and for ten of the twelve monthly lists of 1993.

This audit retracted those eight rows and seven others. Coverage falls to 85.3%.
That is the honest figure and it is still a large gain on the 69.2% that the
JHU + WHO baseline supports on its own, and on the 72.7% the country carried
before this run. Six gap periods remain open, five of them in the 1980s-90s,
and on the evidence assembled by four independent agents the data to close them
does not exist in any public source.

3. SOURCES DISCOVERED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
30 sources at run start, 115 at the end: 85 registered by Agents 1-6.
By reliability level (all 115): Level 1 109, Level 2 4, Level 3 2, Level 4 0.
Of the 85 new: Level 1 81, Level 2 2, Level 3 2, Level 4 0.

The concentration in Level 1 is real, not generous grading. The productive veins
were WHO primary documents (the Weekly Epidemiological Record annual cholera
articles, the weekly "Diseases subject to the Regulations" IHR notification
tables and monthly infected-areas lists, 1971-2001, retrieved as PDFs from the
IRIS DSpace REST API), the Uganda National Institute of Public Health's own
outbreak-investigation series at uniph.go.ug, the Uganda MoH digital library at
library.health.go.ug (55 weekly epidemiological bulletins plus the National
Cholera Prevention and Control Plan and the 2017 Operational Guidelines), the
WHO Global Health Observatory API, and six peer-reviewed papers whose district
tables had to be recovered from printable PDFs because the HTML renderings omit
them.

Zero dead URLs remain in ./data/UGA/metadata_ai.csv. Agent 4 tested all 91 URLs
then present and found exactly one genuine 404 (the retired WHO GHO
indicator-details path, cited by 13 rows); Agent 5 repointed it to the live GHO
API endpoint and re-verified all 13 citing values against it. Agent 5 then swept
all 113. Eight further URLs return 403 or a TLS error to curl but are alive in a
browser or through r.jina.ai. ./data/UGA/attribution_review.csv was never
created, because nothing needed withholding for judgement.

27 registered sources are not cited by any live data row. Five of those
(84, 86, 87, 89, 104) are cited by ./data/UGA/cholera_presence_ai.csv; eight
(40, 42-48) are the WER annual articles behind the retracted zeros and have had
a retraction notice written into their Description so that no future agent
silently rebuilds the same zero from them; the rest are corroborating or
negative-result sources deliberately registered without producing a row.

The single largest contribution is not coverage but resolution. Before this run
the 1997-99 epidemic - the largest in Uganda's record, ~49,500 cases and 2,064
deaths - existed in the AI layer as three coarse rows. It is now tiled
continuously from 28 December 1997 to 27 January 1999 by twelve dated WHO IHR
reporting windows. The 1978-80 epidemic, previously three annual totals, is now
eleven dated windows showing a biphasic 1980 wave. 1991, 1992 and 1994 are
likewise resolved into intra-year windows; 92% of Uganda's 1994 cases fall
between 22 June and 31 August, the exact window of the Rwandan refugee exodus,
which a flat annual number cannot show.

Six calendar years that had no national figure in ANY layer now have one:
1975, 1982, 1995, 1996, 1997 and 2023. 2023 matters most: WHO's own annual
article states that Uganda "did not report cholera data to WHO for 2023", so the
year had no national number anywhere; Uganda's national reference laboratory
confirmed 65 cases. 2023 must never be classified as a zero-transmission year.

MY ASSESSMENT OF WHETHER THE MISSING DATA EXISTS
  1981, 1983 (Jan-Mar), 1984-1986, 1988-1990, 1993 - roughly 8.5 years.
  Probably not, in any public source. Four agents attacked these years by
  different routes: the complete WER annual and weekly archive read in column
  geometry, the WHO GHO API (which holds no Uganda value for any of them), the
  vaccine-preventable-disease return series, PubMed 1970-1999, the Uganda MoH's
  own National Cholera Plan (whose historical narrative offers no 1980s numbers
  and whose figure series starts in 1995), the Karamoja famine literature, the
  1985 refugee-camp literature, colonial-era Protectorate reports, and
  Swahili/regional media. Nothing. If a count exists it is in paper Ugandan
  Ministry of Health annual medical reports of the 1980s, which are not
  digitised. The honest conclusion is that Uganda's cholera record for the Bush
  War decade is lost, not merely unfound - and leaving those years EMPTY tells a
  modeller something true, whereas a zero would tell them something false.

  2019 Karamoja, 2022 district clusters, 2024 Mbale City and Adjumani, and 2025
  Kiryandongo are presence-confirmed but uncounted, and are recorded in
  ./data/UGA/cholera_presence_ai.csv rather than fabricated into counts.

THE UNDER-ASCERTAINMENT RESULT. In 2016, with IDSR running and revitalised,
Uganda's notification to WHO (516 cases, row 127) captured 45% of its own
ministry's published national total (1,156, row 120). That is the WHO
under-ascertainment factor for Uganda IN A GOOD YEAR. Applying anything like it
to 1984-1990 - when Uganda's whole notifiable-disease return was 1.5-6% complete
- finishes the argument for the eight retirements. Agent 6 established the 2016
figure while arbitrating a three-way conflict (516 WHO / 1,156 MoH / 2,316
AJTMH) and it is the more important of the two findings.

CITATION INTEGRITY. Across 184 rows there is now exactly one class of defect
left, and it is disclosed rather than present: 100% of rows resolve to a live

100% carry a reporting_date >= TR, and every CFR matches deaths/sCh to within

9. METHODOLOGICAL FINDINGS WORTH CARRYING TO OTHER COUNTRIES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE WHO IRIS DSPACE REST API is the single highest-value technique from this
   run. /server/api/pid/find?id=hdl:10665/NNNNN -> /core/items/{uuid}/bundles ->
   /core/bundles/{uuid}/bitstreams -> /core/bitstreams/{uuid}/content gives
   direct PDF downloads of every Weekly Epidemiological Record and WHO AFRO
   bulletin back to 1926. The public handle and bitstream URLs return an Angular
   shell, which is why this archive is routinely missed. Send a browser
   User-Agent; the API returns HTML to a bare curl.

2. DOWNLOAD THE PDF BITSTREAM, NOT THE .txt BITSTREAM. IRIS serves an OCR .txt
   alongside each PDF in which the WER's two-column page layout is interleaved
   line by line, so a country name and the numbers on the same printed line
   belong to DIFFERENT countries. Agent 3 correctly refused to extract numbers
   from it; Agent 4 recovered 27 rows from the same documents by running
   `pdftotext -layout` on the PDF. For the hardest cases use
   `pdftotext -bbox-layout` and reconstruct column bands from word coordinates.

3. THE WER INFECTED-AREAS COLUMN TRAP. The monthly "Infected Areas as on ..."
   list is printed in four to six columns and ordered BY DISEASE - Plague, then
   Yellow fever, then Cholera - so a heading in column 1 can sit BELOW, in line
   terms, the entries it governs in column 4. Assigning a country block to the
   nearest heading above it by line number is wrong and produces confident
   nonsense: under the naive method Uganda's "Western Region / Nebbi District"
   entry reads as a CHOLERA listing throughout 1986-2001. It is PLAGUE. Read in
   column order it lands in the plague section every time. This trap cuts both
   ways - the same technique, applied correctly, is what proved the 1981 and
   1993 zeros wrong.

4. `https://r.jina.ai/<full https URL>` reads pages behind Cloudflare that
   return 403 to WebFetch and an interstitial to curl. It opened uniph.go.ug,
   library.health.go.ug and pesacheck.org, which between them produced Uganda's
   most authoritative recent rows. National public health institutes are
   systematically invisible to plain fetching; assume they exist and are blocked
   rather than that they are absent.

5. SCAN CASE-INSENSITIVELY. WER infected-area tables print country names in
   ALL CAPS. Agent 3's first pass over the 1985 and 1986 corpora used a
   case-sensitive pattern and would have missed every hit; the bug was caught
   and corrected mid-run. Assume every archival corpus mixes cases.

6. HARVEST ISSUE INDEXES, NOT SITE SEARCH. uniph.go.ug/?s=cholera is incomplete.
   Harvesting all 40 UPHB volume index pages instead surfaced three cholera
   articles the site search does not return, one of them a district-year absent
   from every layer of the dataset (Nebbi, January-March 2017, 222 cases).

7. CHECK THE ISSUE DATE BEFORE TREATING A "CHOLERA IN {year}" ITEM AS AN ANNUAL.
   IRIS indexes interim reports under the same title. The items a naive search
   returns for 1972 and 1973 tabulate only part of the year; the real full-year
   annuals are separate items (WER 48/30 and WER 49/27).

8. ASYMMETRY OF INFECTED-AREA EVIDENCE. Presence on WHO's cholera infected-areas
   list is strong evidence the country was infected. Absence from it is weak
   evidence of anything: in 1987 Uganda notified 140 cases and 50 deaths while
   appearing on ELEVEN infected-area lists, every one of them for plague and
   none for cholera. Do not build zeros on absence from this list.

9. WHO'S OWN VOCABULARY DISTINGUISHES THE TWO CASES AND SHOULD BE USED AS THE
   TEST. From 2016 the WER annual carries separate lists for "countries
   reporting zero cases" and "countries where data were not available"; the
   2022 annual lists Uganda among 14 African countries that "reported 0 cholera
   cases and deaths in 2022, fulfilling the public health surveillance service
   of 'zero reporting'", while the 2023 annual names Uganda among countries that
   "did not report cholera data to WHO for 2023" - a year in which Uganda
   demonstrably had outbreaks. Before 2020 the format had no way to express
   "reported zero" at all, and across the whole global series (2,469 records,
   1949-2016) only 51 carry an explicit 0, of which exactly one falls in
   1970-1995. A pre-2020 absence from that table is silence. This is the single
   most transferable finding in this report and it almost certainly invalidates
   historical zero rows in other countries built the same way.

10. DEFECTS OUTSIDE THE AI LAYER - REPORTED, NOT FIXED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
These are in read-only or shared files and were deliberately left alone.

A. ./data/UGA/cholera_data_who.csv - THE ZERO-FILL DEFECT IS ALREADY FIXED IN
   THE BUILDER; MY BRIEF'S DESCRIPTION OF IT IS OUT OF DATE.
   The file does carry 74 national weekly rows with a blank sCh, spanning
   2023-07-31 to 2025-03-23. But py/build_weekly_timeseries.py no longer
   zero-fills them: load_national_rows() sets has_count=False for a blank sCh
   and both the weekly and the non-weekly loops skip such rows outright
   ("blank sCh = no count reported; not an observation" / "do NOT emit a zero").
   The current ./data/UGA/cholera_weekly_UGA.csv confirms it - every week from
   August 2023 to December 2024 is either WHO observed or JHU Fourier, and none
   is a documented or inferred zero. No action needed, and a future run should
   not spend effort on it.

B. A REAL BUILDER DEFECT FOUND WHILE CHECKING (A): ROWS WITH A COUNT BUT NO sCh
   ARE SILENTLY DROPPED. has_count is keyed on sCh alone, so a row carrying only
   cCh or only deaths is discarded rather than used. For Uganda this drops:
     AI  row 197  2023  cCh 65   - the ONLY national figure for 2023 anywhere,
                                   in a year WHO records as non-reporting
     AI  row 198  2015  cCh 137
     AI  row 199  2018  cCh 109
     JHU row 1314 2018  deaths 0
     JHU row 1479 2018  deaths 44
   The 2023 loss is material: MOSAIC receives nothing for a year in which
   Uganda's national reference laboratory culture-confirmed 65 cases. Suggested
   fix, for the pipeline owner and not for a country agent: treat a row as
   informative if any of sCh, cCh or deaths is present, and disaggregate on
   whichever is available.

C. ./data/UGA/metadata_jhu.csv - all 55 URLs return HTTP 404, BUT NOT FOR THE
   REASON PREVIOUSLY RECORDED. The stated cause was that ".csv" had been
   stripped and the UID truncated to three digits. That is not what the file
   contains: every URL ends in a full "ObservationCollectionNNNNN.csv", and all
   55 filenames match a file that exists at
   /Users/johngiles/MOSAIC/jhu_cholera_data/data/Uganda/. They 404 because
   github.com/InstituteforDiseaseModeling/jhu_cholera_data is a PRIVATE
   repository and returns 404 to anonymous HTTP at the repository root as well.
   Nothing is malformed and no data is lost; the citations are simply not
   publicly resolvable. A future agent told to "fix the truncated UIDs" would
   find nothing to fix.

D. ./data/UGA/cholera_data_jhu.csv - apparent duplication for 1999 and 2000.
   JHU sums to 10,234 cases / 458 deaths for 1999 and 6,131 / 341 for 2000,
   against WHO's official 5,179 / 228 and 2,807 / 172 - almost exactly double in
   both years. Separately, JHU renders the Moroto 2010 outbreak four times over
   (a summary row of 610, ten weekly rows summing to 542, 65 daily rows summing
   to 541, and a 16 Apr - 22 May row of 220). DO NOT TREAT JHU SUB-NATIONAL SUMS
   AS AN INDEPENDENT RECONSTRUCTION FOR ANY UGANDAN YEAR - this is what made
   Agent 2's "99% reconciled" claim for row 33 a coincidence of double-counting.

E. ./data/UGA/cholera_data_jhu.csv carries a 2022 national zero which this audit
   left standing. On Agent 6's analysis it is defensible: WHO's 2022 annual
   affirmatively lists Uganda as a zero-reporter, the nine AFRO countries
   reporting cholera in 2022 do not include Uganda, and Uganda's own 2026
   elimination paper plots the 2022 bar flat. It sits in direct tension with
   presence rows 2-5 of ./data/UGA/cholera_presence_ai.csv, which record
   significant HMIS district clusters in Amuru, Pakwach/Nebbi, Busia and
   Namayingo in 2022 (Local Moran's I = 0.070, Z = 2.01, p = 0.045 - a statistic
   that cannot be computed on an all-zero surface). The two come from different
   instruments, IDSR notification versus HMIS aggregates, and both should be
   retained WITH that explanation attached rather than silently reconciled.
